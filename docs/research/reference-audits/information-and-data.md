### Current operational rules

**Generation.** The leaf captures decision, audience, source/date, units,
denominator, baseline, uncertainty, missingness, transformations, conclusion,
update path, medium, interaction and accessibility. It preserves supplied
values before choosing an encoding, matches common comparison tasks to visual
forms, asks for one reading thesis, direct labels, truthful scale, uncertainty,
redundant channels and an editable data-to-graphic mapping.

**Critique.** It recomputes totals and changes and inspects truncation,
inconsistent baselines, area/length mismatch, hidden denominators, dual axes,
false precision, missing units, legend travel, category overload, colour-only
distinctions and claims stronger than the data.

**Repair.** It supplies the most important causal priority: repair the encoding
or claim before styling. It does not yet map common failures to their data,
transform, scale, interaction or handoff causes.

**Exception.** Expressive annotation, illustration, distortion and nonstandard
sequencing are allowed when numeric relationships remain truthful and the
decorative layer does not imply data. The compensation test is useful but does
not yet address transformed axes, cartograms, schematic maps, or conventional
diagram notation.

**Verification.** It requires source cross-check, target size, projection,
print, an accessible alternative and an editable mapping. It correctly refuses
statistical, cartographic, scientific or domain-expert validation.

## 2. What is already strong

1. **The decision and evidence precede the chart.** The leaf does not begin
   from a catalogue. Audience, question, source, date, units, denominator,
   baseline, uncertainty, missingness and transformations can all change the
   representation. [L-23]
2. **Supplied values are protected before visual synthesis.** That is a
   binding integrity floor and a useful defense against a polished but altered
   story.
3. **Encoding is tied to the comparison.** Position/length, sequence,
   containment, connection and geography are treated as different semantic
   tasks. This is consistent with foundational graphical-perception and
   expressiveness research while avoiding a total ranking presented as law.
   [L-23; ID-01 through ID-03]
4. **“Map only when geography is causally relevant” is an excellent scope
   check.** It prevents geographic shape from replacing a more precise
   comparison merely because a location field exists. [L-23; ID-09]
5. **One reading thesis counters dashboard/card sprawl.** Direct labels,
   source/context and scale support a coherent argument rather than a wall of
   disconnected metrics. [L-23; ID-14]
6. **Uncertainty and missingness are first-class inputs.** The leaf refuses to
   present every value as equally certain and exact. [L-23; ID-05, ID-06]
7. **Redundant encoding and final-distance labels are functional, not
   decorative, checks.** They support both comparison and access. [L-14, L-23;
   ID-10]
8. **The editable mapping is explicitly preserved.** This is the right handoff
   object: not only pixels, but the relationship among source fields,
   transformations, marks, scales and interactions. [E-17; ID-16]
9. **The critique list targets high-risk deception causes.** Baselines,
   denominators, area/length, dual axes, false precision and claim strength are
   more consequential than generic polish.
10. **The claim ceiling is honest.** Statistical inference, cartography,
    science and domain validity remain external authorities. A render never
    validates the source or analysis.

The simple SOL data-story case and the motion/presentation cases confirm that
the model can produce usable information graphics under strong prompts. They do
not show that a broad checklist improves that ability or that the long-tail
failure families are mastered.

## 3. Missing professional capability

| Capability | Current gap | Required applied capability | Consequence if absent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source, grain and transformation lineage | Source/date and transformations are inputs but there is no reconciliation procedure. | Record source owner, extraction time, grain, filters, joins, denominators, exclusions, imputation, aggregation, derived fields and update path; reconcile displayed values to a reproducible table before design. | A visually correct chart can faithfully render the wrong grain, stale extract or inconsistent definition. |
| Encoding accuracy | The comparison-to-encoding line is too coarse for distributions, correlations, intervals, networks, spatial data and mixed tasks. | Classify field semantics and viewer task; check that marks/channels express all and only the intended facts; prefer more accurately judged channels when precision matters; expose unavoidable perceptual cost. | Area, angle, colour, 3D depth or node size can imply precision or order that the data does not support. |
| Chart versus table versus text | The leaf assumes a form after diagnosis but lacks a no-chart decision. | Use text for one decisive value, a table for exact lookup or many heterogeneous values, and a chart for pattern/comparison. Permit a chart plus table when both overview and exact values matter. | A decorative chart can make lookup slower, while a giant table can hide the pattern. |
| Chart selection | Part-to-whole, change and relation are only a start. | Distinguish amount, ranking, deviation, distribution, correlation, temporal change, part-to-whole, flow, hierarchy, network, geospatial pattern and exact lookup; choose the simplest form that supports the actual task and data types. | Familiar chart names become recipes and compound forms encode incompatible comparisons. |
| Tables | No table-specific design or accessibility exists. | Define row/column semantics, units, source, sort, grouping, decimals/precision, totals/subtotals, missing values, alignment, repeated headers, responsive strategy and programmatic header associations. | Exact values are hard to compare, row/column context is lost, and exported tables become inaccessible. |
| Bar/area baselines | “Truthful scale” and “inconsistent baselines” do not state the causal reason. | When mark length or filled area is measured from a baseline, normally include the meaningful zero so the physical extent remains proportional. If zero is not meaningful or an exception is necessary, change the mark or make the crop and values unmistakable and test the task. | A truncated baseline exaggerates ratios; warnings may not eliminate the effect. [ID-07, ID-08] |
| Line/scatter ranges | The leaf can overcorrect by treating every non-zero axis as deceptive. | Choose a range that reveals the relevant variation while retaining comparison context; state cropped domains, comparable panels and reference values; avoid making slope/volatility claims from an arbitrary aspect ratio. | Starting every line at zero can erase the pattern; aggressive cropping can inflate it. |
| Log and other transformed axes | No rule distinguishes additive from multiplicative comparisons. | Use a log axis only for positive values and a task about ratios/orders of magnitude; label transformation/base and meaningful ticks; make zero/negative handling explicit; offer a linear comparison when audience literacy is uncertain. | Equal screen distances can be misread as equal differences rather than equal ratios. |
| Uncertainty and distributions | Uncertainty is named but not typed or linked to a decision. | Identify source and semantics: variability, measurement error, sampling error, model/posterior uncertainty, forecast range, scenario or data quality. Choose intervals, distributions, ensembles, frequency frames or scenarios for the viewer task; label coverage/assumptions. | An unlabeled error bar can mean several incompatible things, while animation can conceal exact values. |
| Missing, zero, suppressed and not applicable | All are grouped under missingness. | Preserve distinct states in data and display; explain suppression/privacy, imputation and sample changes; never connect missing time points or coerce unavailable values to zero without authority. | Gaps become false declines, false continuity or false precision. |
| Annotation and narrative claims | Annotation is permitted as expression, not as evidence-linked argument. | Tie title, callouts and reference lines to explicit records/transformations; distinguish observation, interpretation, benchmark, event and causal hypothesis; keep annotations synchronized with filters and locale. | A true chart can carry a false causal headline or stale annotation. |
| Maps and normalization | Geography relevance is checked, but projection, normalization, classification and area bias are absent. | Decide map versus non-map; match projection/extent to task; use rates or otherwise defensible derived values for choropleths; use proportional symbols for totals; record denominator, classification, boundary vintage, no-data and uncertainty; inspect small regions and map scale. | A choropleth of raw counts mainly maps population/area, classification invents visual breaks, and boundaries imply false precision. |
| Spatial inference and ethics | No check exists for ecological inference, aggregation effects, geocoding uncertainty or sensitive locations. | Bound conclusions to the mapped unit; distinguish people from areas; disclose aggregation and resolution; consult domain/cartographic authority for projection, boundary and privacy consequences. | Area-level patterns are attributed to individuals, or precise points expose people or resources. |
| Diagram semantics | “Connection for relation” does not define relation, direction, cardinality or notation. | Name node types and scope; define every edge's direction, relation, state and cardinality where relevant; choose a domain notation such as BPMN/UML/C4 only when its semantics apply; supply a key and check paths against the source model. | Arrows become decoration: “uses,” “sends,” “depends on,” sequence and containment are visually conflated. |
| Diagram geometry | Collision is not enough; topology and routing need proof. | Preserve node/edge inventory, avoid ambiguous crossings and false proximity, maintain readable labels, group without implying containment, and render at actual size; repair geometry without changing semantics. | A cleaner layout can silently reverse, merge or omit relationships. |
| Dashboard purpose and state | The leaf criticizes metric cards but lacks a monitoring/analysis/communication model. | Define audience, decision, cadence, freshness, primary outcomes, drivers, guardrails, filters and drill path; reconcile cards/charts/tables under one metric model; expose active filters, incomplete periods, permissions and update failures. | A dashboard looks complete while metrics use different grains, filters or time windows. |
| Interaction | Interaction is an input but no semantic contract exists. | Define what filter, brush, hover, zoom, sort and drill change; preserve context, keyboard access, selection state, reset/recovery, query meaning and share/export state; ensure the default view already answers the primary question. | Hidden hover becomes the only source of values, or a filter changes one panel but not another. |
| Accessibility | “Accessible alternative” is too generic. | Provide a concise purpose/takeaway plus structured detail appropriate to the task: accessible table/data, text description, keyboard exploration, focus/order, programmatic names and redundant encodings. Verify the alternative from the same data/mapping. | A stale alt paragraph or raw CSV is called equivalent even when it cannot support the visual task. |
| Locale formatting | No locale/data-format rule exists. | Use current locale data/API for numbers, decimals, grouping, percentages, currency, dates, units and numbering systems; preserve machine values separately; test long labels and bidirectional strings. | `1,234`, `1.234`, `12/10`, `%` placement, currency and compact notation become ambiguous or wrong. |
| Responsive visualization | Target size is checked only after generation. | Recompose rather than uniformly shrink: resize, reposition, simplify, aggregate, facet, change labels/annotation/interaction, or switch forms while preserving the thesis and mappings; compare device variants together. | Labels disappear, density becomes unreadable, or the mobile version answers a different question accidentally. |
| Print/static handoff | Print is named but no data-specific package exists. | Deliver source data snapshot, transformation/field dictionary, editable/declarative mapping, font/colour assets, static interaction fallback, target dimensions, export profile and render receipts; test grayscale, projection and physical size when relevant. | An interactive tooltip-dependent chart exports without values, or print changes scale/legend/line meaning. |
| Failure-to-repair trace | The current list stops at finding/priority. | For each defect record observation → likely reader error → data/transform/encoding/layout cause → smallest repair → preserved strengths → recalculation/render/access regression. | The agent restyles a symptom, changes the data relationship, or “fixes” a valid exception. |

## 4. Rule-quality audit

### Classification

| Class | Rules that belong here | Override condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binding constraints | Preserve source values and logical relationships; identify source/date/grain/units/denominator/transform; do not invent data, uncertainty, causality or geography; obey applicable accessibility, privacy, notation, platform and print contracts; keep the accessible equivalent and handoff mapping synchronized. | Only an authorized change to data, analysis, rights, compliance, notation or production contract. |
| Evidence-bounded rules | Position/common-scale and length generally support more accurate magnitude judgments than area/angle for tested tasks; truncated bar extents can inflate perceived difference; uncertainty encodings affect specific inference tasks; responsive strategies involve density/message trade-offs. | Different task, population, mark, scale, medium or study evidence. State the boundary instead of turning a ranking into law. |
| Contextual conventions | Bar/line/area forms, baseline, axis range, log scale, table ordering, direct labels, map projection/classification, diagram notation, dashboard hierarchy, annotation style, locale display and print conventions. | Domain standard, audience literacy, publication/tool system or tested deliberate exception supplies a better convention. |
| Heuristics and starting hypotheses | Prefer the simplest form; begin precise magnitude comparison with aligned position/length; include zero for measured bar/area extent; direct-label when feasible; use few high-signal dashboard controls; place source/freshness near consequence; recompose at small widths. | Real content, density, task, uncertainty, interaction, geography, notation, audience or medium disproves the start. |
| Preferences | Pie/donut versus bar, gridline density, label placement, minimal versus expressive annotation, small multiples versus interaction, dashboard tiles, map palette, edge routing style. | Preferences never override data truth, source, access, notation, privacy or production. |
| Justified exceptions | Cropped bar baseline, log scale, dual scales, distortion/cartogram, nonlinear sequence, embellished annotation, animation, schematic geography, deliberate edge crossings. | Purpose is explicit; the transformation/exception is visible; required values and relationships remain recoverable; compensating labels/context exist; the target task tests better; accessible/static alternatives survive. |

### Slogans, cargo-cult numbers and unsupported universals to reject

- “Position is always best.” Graphical-perception rankings are task- and
  comparison-specific, and individual performance varies. Use them as evidence,
  not a complete chart recommender. [ID-01 through ID-03]
- “Every bar chart must start at zero” without describing why. Zero is the
  strong default when viewers compare bar/area extent. Current task-based work
  cautions against calling every truncation equally misleading; any exception
  still needs explicit values/baseline, a defensible task and testing. [ID-07,
  ID-08]
- “Every line chart must start at zero.” Lines encode position/change, not
  filled extent from the axis; range and aspect must serve the stated question
  without hiding context. [ID-05, ID-07]
- “Never use log scales.” Log scales can reveal multiplicative structure and
  orders of magnitude. They require positive-domain handling, explicit ticks,
  ratio language and audience testing. [ID-07]
- “Never use dual axes,” “never use pie charts,” or “always use a table.” These
  are strong risk heuristics in many contexts, not substitutes for the actual
  comparison and alternative design.
- Fixed caps such as five colours, seven categories, four KPI cards, one chart
  per screen, ten gridlines, or a universal dashboard fold. Source-specific
  style-guide numbers are local conventions, not cognitive laws.
- “Maps are more engaging.” A map is warranted by spatial questions, not a
  geography column. Choropleths normally need normalized values, not raw totals.
  [ID-09]
- “A legend makes the chart accessible.” Colour redundancy, structure, labels,
  description, keyboard behavior and an equivalent data/task path are separate.
  [L-14; ID-10, ID-18]
- “Show all uncertainty as error bars” or “HOPs are always clearer.” Viewers
  need the uncertainty meaning and a representation suited to the judgment;
  animated ensembles helped specific ordering tasks, not all tasks. [ID-05,
  ID-06]
- “Declutter everything” or “maximize the data-ink ratio.” Annotation,
  references, uncertainty, structure and explanation can be functional; style
  minimalism is not truth.
- “Every arrow means flow.” Diagram edges can mean sequence, message,
  dependency, containment, association, cardinality, state transition or data
  movement. Use domain semantics or label the custom notation. [ID-12, ID-13]
- “A dashboard improves decisions.” Dashboard purpose, data quality, metric
  model, interaction and organizational use must be evaluated; tiled appearance
  alone proves nothing. [ID-14]
- “Responsive means scale to fit.” Repositioning, simplification, changed
  annotation, alternate interaction and even a form switch can be required.
  [ID-15]

**Landscape conclusion.** Credible specialist mechanisms exist for dashboard
briefing, declarative chart rendering and technical diagrams. The bounded
search found no credible general Agent Skill for professional cartography that
combines normalization, classification, projection, aggregation/uncertainty,
boundary provenance, privacy and map-use testing. Vendor component Skills and
chart libraries can render maps; they are not cartographic judgment. This
absence is bounded, not a claim that no private or differently named Skill
exists.

### Contradictions and evidence limits

- Graphical-perception rankings are reliable foundations for tested magnitude
  judgments but insufficient as a global chart-selection tree. Task, display,
  data density, viewer ability and interaction change outcomes. [ID-01 through
  ID-03]
- The strong zero-baseline rationale for bars/filled areas remains, while 2026
  task-based research cautions against labeling all truncation equally harmful.
  The safe synthesis is a zero default for extent judgments plus a narrowly
  justified, explicit and tested exception—not a reversed universal. [ID-07,
  ID-08]
- Error bars are compact but often misinterpreted; animated HOPs improved some
  comparative uncertainty tasks, not all. No one uncertainty form wins without
  the semantics and judgment. [ID-05, ID-06]
- W3C alternatives establish web access floors; richer screen-reader
  exploration research shows that one paragraph or raw table may not reproduce
  the visual task. [L-14; ID-10, ID-18, ID-19]
- Maps and diagrams are not merely unusual chart types. Their projection,
  aggregation, notation and edge semantics require separate domain evidence.
  [ID-09, ID-12, ID-13]

## 7. Applied Dos and Don’ts

| Concern | Do | Don't | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data lineage | Freeze/reconcile the displayed table with source, date, grain, filters, joins, denominators, exclusions and transformations. Keep an update path. | Do not infer definitions from column names, mix extracts silently, or validate only the final pixels. | L-23, E-17 |
| Encoding | Name viewer task and field semantics; check that the visual grammar expresses all and only the intended facts; use precise channels when precise comparison matters. | Do not apply a total channel ranking or let area, angle, colour, 3D depth or decoration imply unsupported order/magnitude. | ID-01 through ID-03 |
| Chart/table/text choice | Use text for one key value, tables for exact lookup/heterogeneous values, charts for pattern/comparison, and combine overview with detail when required. | Do not chart data merely because a chart was requested if a clearer table/text form satisfies the real outcome; explain the correction. | L-23; bounded professional rationale |
| Tables | Define captions, headers, units, sort, grouping, precision, totals and missing states; align comparable numbers; preserve semantic header associations and responsive/print structure. | Do not use merged visual cells without a navigable relationship, repeat units inconsistently, or encode missing as blank without explanation. | ID-07, ID-10, ID-11 |
| Bar/area baseline | Start from the meaningful zero when physical length/area encodes amount. If a narrow exception is necessary, show values/baseline conspicuously, test ratio judgments, or switch to points/lines. | Do not truncate a bar and rely on a warning mark to undo exaggerated extent. | ID-07, ID-08 |
| Line/scatter range | Choose a range/aspect that reveals the target change and retains reference context; label cropped domains and keep comparable panels consistent. | Do not force zero when it erases the pattern, or crop/aspect-stretch to manufacture volatility. | ID-07; bounded empirical rationale |
| Log axis | Use for positive multiplicative/order-of-magnitude questions; label base/meaningful ticks and zero/negative exclusions; provide a linear comparator when needed. | Do not use log bars or unlabeled nonlinear scales for an audience expected to read additive differences. | ID-07 |
| Uncertainty | State what uncertainty means and its coverage/assumptions; match intervals, distributions, ensembles, scenarios or frequency frames to the judgment; preserve an exact/static route. | Do not show an unlabeled error bar, hide model/data uncertainty, or assume animation is always clearer or accessible. | L-23; ID-05, ID-06 |
| Missingness/precision | Distinguish zero, missing, suppressed, not applicable and imputed; show sample changes; round to source/decision precision. | Do not connect gaps, fabricate zeros, reveal suppressed values or report more digits than the evidence supports. | L-23 |
| Annotation | Link titles, notes, events, targets and callouts to explicit data/context; separate observation from interpretation/causal hypothesis; update annotations with filters. | Do not let a causal headline outrun an associational chart or leave a filtered dashboard with stale claims. | L-23; ID-14 |
| Map selection | Use a map only for a spatial question. Match projection, extent, scale and boundary vintage to the task; compare a non-map alternative when geography mainly adds shape. | Do not map a location field for engagement alone or treat every administrative polygon as an equal visual unit. | L-23; ID-09 |
| Choropleth normalization | Use rates/ratios or another defensible derived measure with explicit denominator; use proportional symbols for totals; show no-data and classification. | Do not colour polygons by raw counts, choose class breaks for drama, or infer individual behavior from area aggregates. | ID-07, ID-09 |
| Diagram edges | Inventory nodes/edges from the source model; name direction/relation/cardinality/state; select exact UML/BPMN/C4 semantics only when applicable; supply a key. | Do not use arrow colour/shape as decoration, collapse unlike directions, or import notation symbols without their contract. | ID-12, ID-13 |
| Diagram repair | Repair routing, collisions and grouping without changing node/edge inventory; rerender at target size and compare paths to the source model. | Do not clean geometry by deleting edges, reversing direction or making proximity imply an absent relation. | ID-12, ID-13; specialist prior art |
| Dashboard | Define purpose, cadence, freshness, metric model, primary outcomes, drivers, guardrails, filters and drill path; reconcile every panel and show active state. | Do not default to four KPI cards, make hover the only detail, or mix current/partial periods and metric grains. | ID-14; OpenAI Skill prior art |
| Interaction | Define each control's data/query effect, accessible operation, state visibility, reset/recovery and export/share behavior; make the default view useful. | Do not add filters/brushes as polish, allow inconsistent cross-filtering, or hide units/source inside a tooltip. | E-17, ID-14, ID-19 |
| Accessibility | Supply purpose/takeaway plus structured detail and data; use redundant channels, semantic tables/labels and keyboard exploration where needed; generate alternatives from the same mapping. | Do not call a legend, raw CSV, stale alt text or inaccessible canvas equivalent by default. | L-14; ID-10, ID-18, ID-19 |
| Locale | Format display values with a declared locale and current CLDR/standard API; test decimal/grouping, percent, currency, date, unit, numbering system, long labels and bidi; retain machine values. | Do not hand-build separators/compact suffixes or use locale-formatted strings as the computation source. | ID-11 |
| Responsive/print handoff | Recompose and compare device variants; preserve thesis/mapping, make static interaction fallbacks, deliver source snapshot, transforms/spec, assets, dimensions and render/preflight receipts. | Do not uniformly shrink, remove “secondary” data without checking the message, or export a tooltip-dependent chart as a static image. | E-17; ID-15, ID-16 |
| Critique/repair/exception | Record observation → reader error → cause → smallest repair → preserved strengths → recalculation/render/access regression. For exceptions, expose transformation and compensation. | Do not fix style before data/encoding, erase expressive success automatically, or claim an exception truthful because values are printed somewhere. | Current Core/leaf; ID-17 |

### Failure → cause → repair map

| Failure | Likely cause | Smallest coherent repair | Regression proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Truncated bar exaggerates difference | Extent encoding detached from meaningful zero | Restore zero or switch to point/range form; keep direct values and context | Ratio/value tasks on render; source reconciliation |
| Line looks flat or explosive | Automatic zero/cropped domain or aspect ratio unrelated to task | Reset domain/aspect around stated comparison; disclose crop/reference | Same data, alternative range comparison; claim review |
| Log chart reads as additive | Transformation/ticks/audience not explained | Label log base/ratio meaning and positive-domain limits; provide linear comparator | Value/ratio reading check with intended audience |
| Map mainly shows population | Raw totals in area fill or wrong denominator | Normalize with defensible rate or use proportional symbols; state denominator | Recompute sample regions; map/non-map comparison |
| Missing period appears as zero/continuous | Null coercion, imputation or connected path | Preserve gap/suppression state; annotate sample change; break the line | Source null-state test and rendered gap |
| Uncertainty looks like decoration | Interval semantics omitted or form mismatched to task | Name uncertainty/coverage and use an interval/distribution/scenario form suited to judgment | Domain/statistical review and task comprehension |
| Diagram arrows are ambiguous | Edge types/direction/cardinality not modeled | Recover edge inventory, label semantics, select exact notation/key | Source-model parity; path/direction review |
| Dashboard numbers disagree | Different grain, filters, time windows or duplicate metric logic | Reconcile to one metric model and expose active filters/freshness | Cross-panel totals and filter-state tests |
| Legend travel and colour-only series | Indirect mapping and insufficient redundant channels | Direct-label/end-label; add line/shape/pattern/order distinction | Grayscale/CVD and final-distance render |

| Locale changes magnitude/date meaning | Hard-coded formatting or compact suffix | Apply declared locale formatter; keep raw value separate | Multi-locale snapshots and exact-value comparison |
| Mobile chart clips/changes message | Uniform shrinking or untracked element deletion | Recompose marks/labels/annotation/interaction with explicit preserved thesis | Side-by-side desktop/mobile mapping and task check |
| Print omits interactive meaning | Tooltip/filter state not represented in static export | Freeze state, add direct/static annotations/table, include source/spec receipt | Target-size PDF/print render and data parity |

## 8. Architecture recommendation

### Considered mechanisms

1. **Deepen the current single leaf.** Lowest router churn, and it may exceed
   the 1,800-token cost target if complete causal instruction is inseparable.
   Here it would still load cartography and diagram notation for an ordinary
   bar chart and would leave different authorities behind one `data_story`
   signal.
2. **Split quantitative visualization from spatial/relational displays.** Better
   than one leaf, but maps and semantic diagrams still share almost no causal
   failure model: projection/normalization versus notation/topology/edge
   semantics.
3. **Split into three direct flat leaves.** Matches the specialist Skill
   landscape and authoritative sources; makes maps and diagrams testable
   without turning chart work into a large catalogue.

### Recommendation: three directly routed flat leaves (`P1`)

Professional coverage and correctness take precedence over token reduction.
The split is justified only because the three task families have independent
routing signals, causal failure models, authorities and test fixtures. It is
not a way to force every leaf under 1,800 tokens. If an applied rule cluster
within any proposed leaf proves inseparable, keep it together and exceed the
cost target; compress only after coverage and selective routing are proven.

**A. `information-design-and-data-visualization`**

- Route: charts, tables, uncertainty, annotation, dashboards, interactive and
  responsive quantitative display, locale formatting, chart access and
  data-to-graphic handoff.
- Owns: `data_encoding`, `data_integrity`, `uncertainty_display`,
  `dashboard_information`, `data_handoff`.
- Initial cost estimate: approximately 1,500–1,750 tokens, to be exceeded when
  complete chart/table/dashboard reasoning cannot be preserved otherwise.

**B. `cartography-and-spatial-data`**

- Route: maps, projections, spatial scale/extent, normalization,
  classification, boundary/source vintage, spatial uncertainty/privacy,
  responsive/print maps and cartographic escalation.
- Owns: `map_decision`, `spatial_encoding`, `map_normalization`,
  `cartographic_proof`.
- Initial cost estimate: approximately 1,250–1,600 tokens, to be exceeded when
  complete cartographic reasoning cannot be preserved otherwise.

**C. `diagrams-and-relational-information`**

- Route: flow, architecture, process, network, hierarchy, ER/UML/BPMN/C4,
  nodes/edges/cardinality/direction, diagram geometry, accessible alternative
  and model-to-diagram handoff.
- Owns: `diagram_semantics`, `relation_encoding`, `edge_contract`,
  `diagram_proof`.
- Initial cost estimate: approximately 1,250–1,600 tokens, to be exceeded when
  complete diagram-semantics reasoning cannot be preserved otherwise.

All three must be direct Core routes with no sibling read or dependency edge.
Each repeats a very small local integrity floor—source/model parity, no invented
facts, accessible/static equivalent and honest evidence—because a hidden common
runtime dependency would violate the flat architecture. Proposed future signals
can distinguish `data_story`, `spatial_story` and `relation_diagram`; W-011 does
not authorize changing the current enum.

With the currently reported 1,283-token Core, a single route would load about
2,533–3,033 tokens. Even an unusual three-leaf task would load approximately
5,283–6,233 tokens, below the 7,000-token mixed-context ceiling. A phased task
remains preferable when statistics, cartography, interaction and production
all require separate authorities.

Ownership boundaries:

- Design owns the communication thesis, visual encoding, hierarchy,
  annotation, map/diagram form and critique.
- Data/statistical/domain owners validate source definitions,
  transformations, inference, uncertainty and conclusions.
- Cartographers/GIS authorities validate projection, aggregation, boundaries,
  spatial inference and map-use conditions.
- UI implements framework interaction, responsive states and platform access
  when active; Design supplies the mapping and intended task.
- Media Production owns export/preflight/provider execution; each leaf supplies
  its data/model-specific handoff and regression contract.
- Sources and Attribution owns the durable source/license registry.

The three-leaf split is an architecture recommendation for W-012, not an edit
to RC7.

## 9. Tests and claim ceiling

### Smallest open Terra High falsifiers

| Case | Seed and task | Falsifies if | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1: chart/table/uncertainty generation | From a supplied immutable table with counts, rates, confidence intervals, missing/suppressed cells, negative change and German/US locale variants, create one public-facing figure plus exact-value route. | Values/denominators change; suppressed becomes zero; chart is chosen over a needed table without reason; bar baseline/log/interval semantics mislead; locale or access alternative diverges; proof is overclaimed. | Recomputed table, declarative spec, source/string parity, locale snapshots, intended-size render, alternative-data parity, statistical/domain and information-design review. |
| G2: cartographic generation | From supplied region counts, populations, boundary vintage and uncertainty, choose map or non-map and produce a print/web comparison. | Raw counts become choropleth fill, projection/class breaks are unexamined, no-data disappears, ecological claim appears, or geography is used when a ranked chart is clearer. | Rate recomputation, boundary/projection/classification record, source/map parity, map and non-map render, cartographer/domain review. |
| G3: relational diagram generation | Convert a supplied process/system model with typed nodes, directed edge kinds, cardinalities and exceptions into a diagram and accessible structured explanation. | An edge is omitted/reversed/merged, notation semantics are invented, containment is implied by proximity, or a visually clean render contradicts the model. | Node/edge inventory diff, notation/key validation, geometry/render inspection, accessible path parity, domain-model owner review. |
| C1: misleading dashboard critique | Read-only critique of a rendered/source dashboard seeded with mixed grains/windows, partial period, stale annotation, truncated bar, unclear log line, dual scale, hover-only values, inaccessible filters and locale mismatch. | Findings remain generic; data/transform causes are missed; every non-zero line is condemned; repair is styling-only; or uninspected source/render facts are claimed. | Localized findings, observation/effect/cause/repair trace, source queries/spec, filter-state screenshots, calculation and access evidence. |
| R1: responsive chart repair | Repair a supplied desktop/mobile Vega-Lite/HTML visualization with clipped labels, lost uncertainty, colour-only series and a tooltip-dependent mobile state while preserving the thesis and data. | It only shrinks, deletes a necessary series, changes transforms, hides values, loses keyboard/alternative access or lacks a before/after mapping. | Spec diff, data checksum, desktop/mobile renders, forced narrow widths, keyboard/screen-reader task, recalculation. |
| R2: static/print handoff repair | Repair a supplied interactive dashboard export whose PDF lacks filter state, source/freshness, exact values, uncertainty and grayscale distinction. | Screenshot is called production-ready, interactive meaning is lost, values change, or manual annotations cannot be regenerated. | Frozen state/data/spec bundle, PDF syntax/preflight, grayscale and projection/target-size renders, table/description parity, print/provider review where required. |

Run changed routes one open Terra High case at a time. Compare the smallest
selected-expert payload with Core-only and only the necessary comparator. A
payload is useful only if it repairs the target failure without regressing
source truth, ownership, access, evidence honesty or context cost. Do not score
“more findings” as better critique.

### Deterministic checks

- immutable input table/model checksum and complete required-field inventory;
- displayed values, aggregates, totals, deltas, rates, denominators, intervals,
  suppression and missingness recomputed from the supplied source;
- source/grain/filter/join/time-window/transform record resolves every mark,
  label, annotation, map class and dashboard metric;
- declarative or editable mapping parses and identifies field → transform →
  mark/channel/scale/projection/interaction;
- bar/area extent baseline and axis domain inspected; log domains contain only
  permitted values and label the transform; panel scales are comparable where
  claimed;
- map projection, extent, boundary vintage, normalization denominator,
  classification, no-data and source attribution recorded;
- diagram node/edge/direction/type/cardinality inventory equals the source
  model; marker/legend/notation references resolve;
- locale snapshots preserve raw values while correctly formatting numbers,
  percentages, currencies, dates, units and numbering systems;
- accessible description/table/keyboard structure derives from the same
  snapshot and supports the stated task; no information relies on colour or
  hover alone;
- desktop/mobile/print variants preserve declared thesis and mappings; source
  validity, clipping, overlap, legend/label fit and static interaction fallback
  pass;
- evidence labels match actual source inspection, calculation, render,
  interaction, preflight and human/domain review.

Deterministic checks can prove value/model parity, explicit transformations,
syntax, geometry, labels, state and recorded metadata. They cannot prove that
the selected statistic is valid, the chart is easiest to understand, a map is
cartographically appropriate, an annotation is fair, a diagram matches
unstated reality, or a dashboard improves decisions.

### Required human/domain authority

- data owner/analyst for source definitions, grain, transformations and metric
  reconciliation;
- statistician or subject-matter expert for inference, uncertainty, log/ratio
  interpretation and causal claims;
- qualified information designer for encoding, hierarchy, annotation, repair
  and expressive exception judgment;
- cartographer/GIS and affected-domain authority for projections,
  normalization, classification, boundaries, spatial privacy and map use;
- model/process owner for diagram semantics and completeness;
- accessibility specialists and disabled users when equivalence or interaction
  is consequential;
- platform/BI/UI owner for filter, refresh, permissions and sharing behavior;
- print/projection/provider owner for production acceptance.

### Claim ceiling after passing

Passing these open cases can support only that the changed leaves improved the
tested generation, critique and repair behaviors for the recorded data,
notations, media, languages, tools and reviewers. It cannot support:

- statistical, scientific, financial, medical or domain correctness;
- universal encoding rankings, optimal chart choice or comprehension;
- one mandatory bar/line/log/dual-axis/pie/dashboard rule for all tasks;
- cartographic expertise, neutral maps, valid individual-level inference or
  coverage of every projection/geography;
- complete UML/BPMN/C4 or general diagram-modeling mastery;
- accessibility for all users, assistive technologies or interaction tasks;
- production proof outside the exact browser/BI/PDF/printer/projector tested;
- improved organizational decisions, preference consensus, expert equivalence,
  market leadership or lower total cost.

## 10. Priority

**Overall priority: `P1`.** The current leaf is a safe retained floor and its
explicit statistical/cartographic/domain boundary prevents a general `P0`
classification. Its missing applied depth nevertheless blocks the all-round
designer goal for charts, tables, uncertainty, maps, diagrams, dashboards,
access and handoff.

| Priority | Gap clusters | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Source/grain/transform lineage; chart/table/text decision; encoding/scale/baseline/log diagnosis; uncertainty/missingness; map normalization/projection; diagram edge semantics; dashboard metric state; accessible equivalents; locale; responsive/static handoff; causal repair | These determine whether the visual communicates the supplied evidence truthfully and can be regenerated, understood and handed off. |
| P2 | Deeper annotation/narrative craft, richer interaction patterns, advanced distributions/networks, map aesthetics, diagram geometry optimization and house-style governance | Useful quality/efficiency depth after the P1 truth-and-proof spine exists. |
| P3 | Large chart-type catalogues, historical forms, branded themes or additional style examples without an observed outcome gap | Enrichment does not qualify professional capability and can encourage recipe selection. |

Conditional escalation: a live high-stakes public, medical, financial,
scientific, electoral, safety, privacy-sensitive or operational dashboard/map
task is `P0` if source/domain/statistical/cartographic/access authority or
required proof is absent. The Skill must stop, narrow the claim or request the
authority; visual confidence cannot compensate.

### Open limits

- No dataset, chart, table, map, diagram, dashboard, locale, browser, BI tool,
  PDF or physical print was executed in this audit.
- No statistician, cartographer, domain-model owner, disabled user or print
  provider reviewed an artifact.
- The public Skill search is bounded and may miss private, newly published or
  differently named cartography/information-design Skills.
- Several foundational and professional sources are copyrighted,
  abstract-level or reference-only; their expression must not be copied into
  the MIT package.
- Visualization research is task-, population-, display- and chart-specific;
  older perceptual rankings and newer corrective studies must remain bounded.
- CLDR, ECMA-402, WCAG/WAI material, tool/library behavior, map boundaries,
  BI platforms and professional guidance are mutable and require current
  verification before release or production claims.
