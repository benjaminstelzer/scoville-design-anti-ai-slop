## 1. Current contract

### Activation, ownership, status, sources, and measured size

The Direct Index activates this leaf when a design invokes a living culture, community, identity, sacred or political symbol, stereotype risk, vulnerable participant, personal data, sustainability claim, AI-generated evidence, rights-sensitive asset, or jurisdictional duty. The module registry exposes six independently legible signals: **culture_risk**, **rights_provenance**, **privacy_risk**, **sustainability_claim**, **synthetic_evidence**, and **jurisdiction_duty**. The single current leaf owns **authority_boundary**, **representation_risk**, **consent_privacy**, and **social_environmental_risk**.

Its status is **retained-floor** and its intervention class is **external-verification**. The declared sources are L-10, L-18, L-19, L-30, L-39, and L-40. The file measures 2,572 UTF-8 bytes and 498 o200k_base tokens with the repository-pinned SkillOpt tokenizer. This is far below the 1,800-token cost target, but size is not evidence of sufficient applied coverage.

The module admission ledger cites the imagery-system baseline as SOL evidence and explicitly says there is no cross-cultural qualification. Package-forward validation shows that the imagery-system case co-loaded Imagery, Brand, and Culture/Provenance. That proves package routing and a professional risk floor only. The baseline did not test a real affected-community authority path, contested place names or borders, sacred or secret material, child/bystander release decisions, public-file metadata leakage, multi-layer asset clearance, documentary-versus-synthetic integrity, or substantiated environmental claims. The Terra transfer report contains no leaf-specific Culture call, and RC7 proves signal stability rather than domain outcomes. The domain maturity record therefore correctly limits the claim to identifying risk, missing local evidence, authority, and consultation.

### Existing generation contract

The leaf already requires an authority map of commissioner, depicted people, speakers, approvers, affected parties, asset owners, and relevant lived, local, legal, or technical authorities. It separates supplied fact, public source, participant testimony, designer inference, and unresolved claim. Generation must use specific verified context, preserve participant agency, avoid invented heritage/authenticity/endorsement/environmental benefit, provide a consultation point where model knowledge is not authority, and record asset origin, transformations, consent/rights status, and disclosure needs without unnecessary personal data.

### Existing critique and repair contract

Critique already looks for stereotype shortcuts, exoticization, erasure, decontextualized sacred or political forms, demographic checklist casting, false documentary cues, privacy leakage, inaccessible participation, unsupported sustainability claims, and power hidden behind aesthetic neutrality. Its best repair rule is causal: repair the premise, source, or authority gap rather than polishing the surface representation.

### Existing exception and verification contract

Quotation, parody, remix, appropriation, historical reference, and synthetic imagery receive context-specific rights and consequence review. Transformative intent or visual quality is not treated as permission. Verification correctly bounds L-30 to Australian packaging/lifecycle context, C2PA to association and tamper history rather than truth or rights, and current legal sources to their jurisdictions. Consequential cases escalate to affected community, native-language, legal, privacy, accessibility, or production authority. The leaf expressly forbids self-certifying cultural safety.

### Current ownership boundary

This leaf is a **risk detector and authority router**, not a substitute for community authority, participant consent, counsel, a data protection officer, safeguarding lead, names or mapping authority, documentary editor, rights-clearance producer, lifecycle assessor, regulator, or certification body. It may block release while a consequential state is unresolved. It must not decide a disputed boundary, invent a community mandate, infer a licence from availability, certify a green claim, or turn a valid provenance signature into truth.

## 2. What is already strong

1. **Authority precedes aesthetic choice.** Asking who speaks, approves, is affected, and owns the input prevents the commissioner or model from silently becoming cultural authority.
2. **Evidence classes stay distinct.** Supplied fact, public source, participant testimony, designer inference, and unknown are not flattened into one confidence-free narrative.
3. **The design subject retains agency.** “Consequential roles and agency, not visual tokens” is more operational than a diversity checklist and protects against a person being used as a demographic sign.
4. **Invented authenticity is prohibited.** The leaf blocks made-up heritage, archival status, community endorsement, and environmental benefit instead of merely requesting “authentic” output.
5. **Consultation is a production stage.** It asks for a point where the relevant authority can influence the work, not a ceremonial review after the design is frozen.
6. **Risk classes are usefully broad.** Consent, privacy, publicity, copyright, trademark, cultural protocol, access, safety, environmental consequence, and distribution are all visible.
7. **Critique names representational mechanisms.** Stereotyping, token casting, exoticization, erasure, and decontextualization are more diagnostic than a generic “be inclusive” command.
8. **Repair targets causes.** Changing the premise, source, authority, or selection can resolve a harmful design that cannot be fixed with a caption, crop, or more polished styling.
9. **Exceptions are not automatic permissions.** Parody, quotation, remix, appropriation, historical reference, and synthetic production remain scoped by rights and consequence.
10. **Provenance is bounded.** C2PA is not confused with factuality, permission, authorship, consent, or quality.
11. **Current-law claims are deferred.** The leaf acknowledges jurisdiction and volatility rather than shipping static legal conclusions.
12. **The cultural-safety ceiling is explicit.** This is essential. Generic model knowledge cannot certify that a living community considers a design safe or legitimate.

These are load-bearing floors. A successor must deepen the decision records and stop conditions without replacing them with country recipes, cultural trait lists, demographic quotas, legal boilerplate, or generic empathy language.

## 3. Missing professional capability

### Authority, mandate, consultation, and disagreement

“Affected community” is not a single approver. The leaf needs to distinguish a rights holder or custodian; a participant speaking for themself; a community-selected representative; a language or names authority; an artist or collaborator; a commissioner; and a legal, privacy, safeguarding, or production owner. Their mandates may overlap or conflict. A visible member of a community, an employee, a cultural consultant, or a public institution must not be presumed to speak for all people or to authorize restricted material.

The minimum consultation record is: concern and affected group; who selected the authority and what mandate they hold; what materials and contexts they may review; confidentiality or sacred/secret restrictions; when review occurs; what can still change; decision, conditions, dissent, and unresolved state; attribution and benefit-sharing terms where applicable; distribution scope; renewal or withdrawal point; and accountable project owner. Consultation must be early enough to change premise, source, credit, access, compensation, or release. A late “looks fine” sign-off is not shared authority.

UNDRIP and living First Nations protocols support self-determination, control, consent, integrity, secrecy, attribution, benefit, and continuity in their stated contexts. They do not license a universal one-form consent ritual for every culture. The runtime must teach how to locate the relevant authority, preserve unknowns, and stop; it must not teach generalized profiles of national, ethnic, religious, or Indigenous behavior.

### Stereotype, tokenism, exoticization, and erasure diagnosis

The current nouns need observable failure signatures:

- a person or group appears only as a demographic marker, victim, helper, ornament, or proof of institutional virtue;
- cultural identity is communicated through interchangeable costume, pattern, color, landscape, food, gesture, or spirituality instead of the actual subject and context;
- multiple living peoples, regions, periods, or practices are collapsed into one timeless style;
- visual difference is intensified for spectacle while ordinary work, expertise, humor, conflict, and self-definition disappear;
- one visible person is made to represent a population, or a “balanced” casting grid removes individual roles and power;
- material from a dominant archive is treated as neutral while community names, authors, languages, protocols, or contested histories vanish;
- a positive stereotype still fixes competence, temperament, family role, spirituality, or body in advance.

Critique must localize the exact asset, crop, caption, casting role, motif, hierarchy, omission, or juxtaposition; name its likely authority/source failure; state the representational and distribution effect; and propose the smallest repair. “This may be culturally insensitive” is unverifiable and not a repair.

### Sacred, secret, ceremonial, and political material

Every motif, name, story, recording, object, dress element, ceremony, site, or symbol that may be sacred, secret, funerary, political, restricted by role, or inappropriate for commercial/public reproduction needs an asset-level state: source; named custodian or rights holder; meaning and intended use; public availability versus permission to reuse; audience, place, timing, medium, adaptation, and commercial restrictions; attribution; benefit; confidentiality; allowed transformations; and review owner. Search-engine availability and museum display do not establish permission.

Unknown authority, unclear access status, or a credible secret/sacred concern must block creation and publication of the material itself. The smallest repair is usually replacement with verified public material, a community-authored contribution, a nonrepresentational structure, or removal. Stylization, parody, a disclaimer, or “inspired by” wording does not cure an authority gap.

Political forms require the same contextual record plus current jurisdiction and campaign policy. A flag, emblem, official seal, party mark, protest symbol, or disputed historical form can carry state, trademark, electoral, safety, or endorsement consequences. WIPO Article 6ter protects specific state emblems and official signs against unauthorized trademark registration/use but does not create a general ownership rule for every depiction. The agent must identify the actual symbol and route the intended use rather than announce a universal ban or clearance.

### Maps, boundaries, flags, country labels, and place names

A cartographic or place-name decision needs: communication purpose and audience; publisher and distribution jurisdictions; map/data version and date; feature IDs; official or commissioned names authority; language/script and endonym/exonym policy; boundary status; disputed claimants and required portrayal; level of detail; labels, notes, legends, disclaimers, and alternate local editions; and accountable geospatial/legal/editorial owners. ISO 3166 supplies codes and draws country names from UN sources; it is not authority for a boundary or political position. UNGEGN supports national names authorities and multilingual, culturally informed naming, but is not an arbitrator for disputed names.

A generic “boundaries and names do not imply endorsement” note documents a position; it does not choose the correct boundary, label, color, omission, or edition. The design must never silently normalize one contested representation. Where authorities disagree, record the conflict, intended audience, instruction source, alternatives considered, and escalation decision. Protect labels, diacritics, scripts, inset maps, dashed/disputed lines, legends, and disclaimers across crop, translation, responsive, and print outputs.

### Consent, minors, bystanders, publicity, and downstream harm

Consent needs a **use matrix**, not one boolean. Record participant identity separately from public display name; participant role and capacity; child’s view where relevant; guardian or responsible-adult authority where applicable; exact capture/interview and whether anything was staged; purpose, narrative, caption, crop, edits, medium, audience, territory, duration, paid promotion, sublicensing, sensitive context, and synthetic manipulation; compensation; withdrawal/renewal route; evidence holder; and unresolved conditions. Legal basis for processing is not always consent, and GDPR Article 8’s age thresholds concern a scoped information-society-service condition, not a global image-release rule.

Ethical permission can be narrower than contractual permission. Consider coercion, dependency, stigma, safety, re-identification, changed political conditions, future searchability, model training, and harm to family or peers. UNICEF’s reporting guidance is child-journalism guidance, not a global commercial release statute, but its best-interest, child voice, guardian, no-staging, context, and harm-even-after-obscuring tests are strong safeguarding inputs. A blurred face can remain identifiable from uniform, location, voice, body, companions, metadata, or story.

Bystanders require current jurisdiction, venue, audience, centrality, sensitivity, expectation of privacy, and intended use review. “Photographed in public” is never a universal permission. For a crowd, use distance, framing, timing, masking, reselection, notice/consent where appropriate, or a non-identifying alternative; do not fabricate an all-purpose release.

### Embedded metadata and public-file privacy

The leaf says not to expose unnecessary personal data but does not require inspection of the delivered file. A public-derivative gate must enumerate visible content plus Exif, IPTC/XMP, C2PA assertions, file properties, filenames, alt/caption text, URLs, manifests, and sidecars for exact GPS, place, capture time, device serial/ID, creator/contact, subject names, AI prompt, prompt-writer identity, account or workflow identifiers, thumbnails, and edit history.

Do not “strip all metadata” by default. Rights, attribution, accessibility, provenance, and workflow metadata may be necessary, while precise location, device, prompt, or participant data may create harm. Keep a controlled master and canonical asset register; create a distribution-specific derivative; retain only fields needed for the declared purpose; generalize, omit, or move sensitive data to controlled storage; then inspect the actual public artifact after export. CIPA Exif 3.1 and IPTC 2025.1 describe capabilities, not a privacy approval. IPTC explicitly supports people, location, products, rights, and new AI prompt/system fields; those fields can help provenance and can leak.

### Third-party marks, products, artwork, buildings, and layered rights

An asset receipt must distinguish the capture creator, copyright owner, licensor, commissioned-work terms, depicted people, private property/site conditions, architecture, public artwork, posters, maps, product design, packaging, trademarks, music/text where present, releases, limitations/exceptions analysis, territory, medium, duration, modification, credit, downstream distributor conditions, and current verification owner. A stock licence to the photograph does not necessarily clear its depicted person, artwork, product, building, logo, or location. Buying the physical object does not transfer its copyright.

Centrality, legibility, narrative context, endorsement/confusion, disparagement, jurisdiction, public-space exceptions, editorial versus advertising use, and transformation can change the assessment. The design agent should inventory and stop; legal or rights-clearance authority decides whether permission, exception, replacement, crop, fictionalization, or recordkeeping is sufficient.

### Documentary, editorial, constructed, and synthetic integrity

Before capture, selection, edit, or generation, declare the mode:

1. documentary/news/evidence;
2. commissioned but observed nonfiction;
3. constructed portrait or demonstration;
4. disclosed reenactment;
5. explanatory illustration or diagram;
6. synthetic concept or fiction.

For each mode record allowed capture direction, content edits, tonal/color edits, compositing, crop/order/caption rules, source retention, disclosure, provenance, and governing publisher/destination policy. Documentary integrity can be changed by staging, removal, generative fill, reconstruction, a crop that hides consequential evidence, or sequencing that invents causality even when a single frame looks plausible. A constructed or synthetic image can be legitimate when its role is clear; it cannot silently inherit documentary cues.

C2PA credentials can bind signed assertions, actions, ingredients, and validation status to an asset. They do not prove truth, accuracy, permission, authorship, consent, identity, or quality; the chain may be incomplete or removed; and absence is not proof of deception. Editing IPTC/XMP after signing can also invalidate credentials. Verification therefore combines original/source comparison, publisher/editor policy, asset register, rights/consent state, disclosure in delivered context, credential validation when present, and human factual review.

### Sustainability claim design

Environmental meaning can be expressed in words, numbers, seals, icons, colors, plants, landscapes, recycled-paper simulation, restrained “natural” photography, or relative hierarchy. The design agent must treat express and reasonably implied environmental benefit as claims. The claim register needs exact wording/visual implication; product/service/process scope; lifecycle stage; comparison baseline and date; geography; measurement method and uncertainty; evidence source/version/date; significant tradeoffs or omitted stages; certification scheme and relationship; expiry/update trigger; claim and legal owners; and required adjacent qualification.

Broad “green,” “eco-friendly,” “sustainable,” “clean,” “climate-friendly,” or zero-impact impressions are not repaired with a footnote when the overall design still implies more than the evidence. Prefer the narrow supported fact, make scope and denominator visible, avoid insignificant-benefit emphasis, and remove unverified seals or nature-coded superiority. ISO 14021:2026 covers self-declared product claims in words, symbols, graphics, labels, advertising, and digital contexts; it is not a universal law or proof of compliance. UK CMA, FTC, EU, and Australian packaging guidance remain jurisdictional. As of this audit, EU Directive 2024/825’s application date of 27 September 2026 is still future; do not describe it as already applicable everywhere.

### Escalation and release state

The authority map needs explicit outcomes: **cleared with evidence**, **cleared with conditions**, **unresolved**, **rejected**, **not applicable**, or **not evaluated**. Unknown is not low risk. Publication must stop when the design depends on unverified sacred/secret authority; vulnerable-person safety or use permission; a disputed map/name decision without publisher authority; consequential personal metadata; a missing rights layer; a false documentary role; or an environmental claim without adequate evidence. Escalation must name the question, affected artifact, needed authority, latest safe point, and fallback—not simply “ask legal.”

## 4. Rule-quality audit

| Rule or claim | Class | Audit judgment | Professional replacement, scope, or exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| Never self-certify cultural safety | Binding epistemic constraint | Keep and make central. | Report verified authorities, evidence, decisions, and unresolved states only. Even community consultation supports the scoped use, not universal safety. |
| Model knowledge is not community authority | Binding ownership constraint | Keep. | Use model knowledge to identify questions and public sources, never to impersonate a custodian, participant, or names authority. |
| Consultation is enough if one community member approves | Unsupported universal | Reject. | Record how the reviewer was selected, their mandate, affected material, dissent, stage, conditions, and continuing decision rights. |
| Consult every member of an affected culture | Impossible slogan | Reject. | Identify legitimate, project-specific authority and affected parties; document scope and disagreement rather than claiming unanimity. |
| Use specific verified context rather than generic markers | Evidence-bounded design rule | Keep and operationalize. | Specify roles, time, place, authorship, names, language, material source, and authority. When specificity would expose restricted/private information, omit it and preserve the limitation. |
| Avoid stereotypes | Necessary goal but under-specified | Refine. | Localize the role, motif, crop, omission, hierarchy, caption, or juxtaposition that fixes a group; repair its source, agency, context, or premise. |
| Positive stereotypes are harmless | False ethical claim | Reject. | Positive labels can still constrain role, competence, temperament, or identity. Use the actual person and task, not group essence. |
| A representative casting checklist prevents tokenism | Cargo-cult heuristic | Reject. | Test agency, role, power, narrative consequence, range, and who defines representation. A count is input evidence, not adequacy proof. |
| Publicly visible cultural material is reusable | False permission claim | Reject. | Public availability differs from permission, sacred/secret status, adaptation, commercial use, attribution, and benefit conditions. |
| Transformative intent, homage, parody, or educational purpose clears the use | False legal/ethical universal | Reject. | Record jurisdiction, exact use, source, rights, authority, consequence, and current counsel. Transformative intent may matter but does not self-authorize. |
| A disclaimer makes a disputed map neutral | False repair | Reject. | Use the publisher’s current authority, label the actual dispute, preserve cartographic conventions, consider local editions, and record unresolved conflict. |
| ISO country codes decide country names or borders | False technical inference | Reject. | ISO 3166 supplies codes; use authoritative names and cartographic/publisher sources for the scoped decision. |
| Flags and state emblems are always public domain and unrestricted | False legal universal | Reject. | Identify the exact emblem and use. Article 6ter, trademark, electoral, advertising, protocol, and local law can apply differently. |
| Consent is one signed release | False process rule | Reject. | Match permission and lawful basis to exact capture, story, crop/edit, media, audience, duration, territory, promotion, sensitivity, and synthetic use. |
| Consent alone makes a portrayal ethical | False ethical proof | Reject. | Also evaluate capacity, coercion, dignity, safety, stigma, downstream discoverability, changed context, and effect on others. |
| A parent’s permission always settles a child’s portrayal | False safeguarding universal | Reject. | Include the child’s views and best interests, guardian authority, risk, jurisdiction, publisher policy, and a child-safeguarding owner. |
| A blurred face is anonymous | Unsupported technical assumption | Reject. | Test voice, clothing, companions, place, timing, metadata, caption, background, and story for re-identification. |
| A public-place photograph is free for advertising use | False legal universal | Reject. | Check jurisdiction, expectation, centrality, venue terms, personality/publicity/privacy, and destination; obtain permission, reframe, or replace when required. |
| Strip all metadata before delivery | Risky heuristic | Reject as universal. | Make a field-level public derivative that minimizes personal/location/device/prompt data while preserving required rights, attribution, accessibility, and provenance elsewhere. |
| Preserve every metadata field for provenance | Risky heuristic | Reject as universal. | Provenance value does not override privacy, safety, or confidentiality; retain only purpose-necessary public fields and controlled evidence. |
| A stock or photographer licence clears everything depicted | False rights claim | Reject. | Inventory people, property, artwork, architecture, products, marks, text, maps, music, and distributor policy as separate layers. |
| A valid C2PA credential proves a real, truthful, licensed image | False proof claim | Reject. | It can support signed provenance/ingredient/action association and validation only. Verify fact, rights, consent, identity, and editorial role separately. |
| No Content Credentials means the asset is deceptive | False negative proof | Reject. | Provenance can be absent, removed, or unsupported. Treat absence as unknown, not guilt. |
| All image edits are forbidden in documentary work | Overbroad convention | Refine. | The named publisher/destination policy controls allowed tonal, crop, dust, caption, and technical operations. Content-changing edits and staging require a different declared mode or rejection. |
| Labeling an image “AI” resolves integrity | Incomplete disclosure heuristic | Reject. | State its actual communicative role and material use of generation/editing where required; keep source, ingredients, facts, consent, rights, and current law separate. |
| Nature imagery and green color are decorative, not claims | False design assumption | Reject. | Evaluate overall implied environmental meaning; visual hierarchy, seals, color, and imagery may extend the claim beyond the words. |
| A specific true environmental fact cannot mislead | False claim rule | Reject. | Check scope, denominator, lifecycle stage, significance, tradeoffs, baseline, time, and overall impression. |
| ISO 14021 or a certification logo proves that a claim is legal everywhere | False authorization | Reject. | Apply the exact scheme/standard and current jurisdiction, verify certificate scope/status and evidence, and preserve legal/claim-owner signoff. |
| Ethical design means avoiding all offense | Preference masquerading as rule | Reject. | Design may intentionally challenge power or represent conflict. Protect truth, authority, participants, access, and consequence; do not promise universal comfort. |
| Deliberate provocation, reclamation, quotation, or satire is exempt | False exception | Reject. | Keep declared intent, speaker authority, target, context, rights, likely affected audiences, safety, access, and compensating evidence. |
| Unknown authority or rights means probably acceptable if risk seems small | Binding release failure | Reject. | Unknown remains unresolved. Replace, remove, narrow the use, or wait for named authority. |

Consequential findings should use **artifact/location → observation → effect and affected party → likely source/authority/process cause → smallest coherent repair → required evidence/owner**. Labels such as unethical, offensive, appropriative, unsafe, greenwashing, or misleading are verdicts only after the evidence and scoped authority are stated.

### Prior-art conclusion

The best adaptable mechanisms are: explicit authority and uncertainty from localize; data-flow and false-positive discipline from secpriv; draft/approval/jurisdiction state from the PIA Skill; sign-then-validate from sign-ai-media; and independent routing from the sustainability audit. They must be recombined with stricter release gates and source ownership. The search found no basis to copy a generic cultural rulebook, assert cultural safety, or use a public Skill as legal or community authority.

### Research synthesis

The sources agree on a narrow operational principle: consequential cultural, participant, rights, privacy, provenance, cartographic, and environmental decisions require the **right scoped authority plus inspectable evidence**. They do not support one “ethical design” score. They also conflict by scope rather than substance: child journalism guidance is not GDPR; a First Nations protocol is not a world-culture template; ISO is not consumer law; a UN names body does not resolve territorial disputes; C2PA is not documentary truth; and a rights-clearance guide is not a legal opinion. The executable should preserve these separations.

## 8. Architecture recommendation

### Bounded mechanisms generated before public comparison

1. **One authority-and-risk manifest compiler.** Keep one leaf and deepen it with a single manifest for culture, people, privacy, rights, provenance, maps, documentary mode, and sustainability.
2. **Three directly routed flat leaves.** Split into **culture-and-representation**, **rights-privacy-and-provenance**, and **sustainability-claims**. Each carries its own minimum authority/evidence/release gate; the design record is their handoff, not an expert dependency.
3. **Keep the 498-token retained floor and route all detail externally.** Preserve the current warnings and rely on model knowledge plus human specialists for every applied decision.

Mechanism 3 is too weak: it names risks but does not prevent metadata disclosure, map normalization, layered-clearance failure, documentary false proof, or environmental part-to-whole claims. Mechanism 1 keeps causal context together but forces unrelated cultural, privacy/provenance, and environmental material into every activation. More detail would make that overread worse.

### Recommendation: split into three flat leaves after authorized successor work

The existing registry already provides the independent routing signals required by the method:

- **culture-and-representation**: culture_risk; owns community authority, representation, living-culture context, sacred/secret/political material, and map/name cultural decisions;
- **rights-privacy-and-provenance**: rights_provenance, privacy_risk, synthetic_evidence, and scoped jurisdiction_duty; owns person/use matrix, public-derivative metadata, layered asset receipt, documentary/synthetic mode, and provenance claim boundary;
- **sustainability-claims**: sustainability_claim; owns express/implied environmental claim register, evidence/lifecycle scope, qualification, certification state, and current jurisdiction review.

Maps, flags, and official marks can intentionally co-load the first two leaves: Culture owns affected authority, meaning, names, and political context; Rights/Privacy/Provenance owns official-mark, jurisdiction, distribution, and clearance state. Synthetic representation of a living culture can also co-load both. A plain environmental claim does not need cultural protocol or C2PA material, and a participant metadata audit does not need lifecycle-claim rules. These are real selection differences, not topic labels invented to reduce tokens.

### Size, frequency, co-loads, and compression boundary

The current combined leaf is 498 tokens. Expected production frequency is unknown; the only package trace shows one imagery-system co-load, not frequency or independent selection. Common future combinations are expected to be:

- Culture + Imagery + Brief/Style for community-facing art direction;
- Culture + Typography for language/script and names review;
- Culture + Information/Data for maps and geospatial claims;
- Rights/Privacy/Provenance + Imagery + Sources for people and assets;
- Rights/Privacy/Provenance + Media Production for public derivatives, manifests, and release;
- Sustainability Claims + Brand/Packaging/Media/Sources for campaign, package, or product claims.

No successor payload has yet been tested, so there is **no measured smallest non-inferior payload**. Coverage must be authored first, then open generation/critique/repair cases should remove only repetition and stable model-prior exposition. The 1,800-token number remains a cost target per leaf, not a reason to omit the authority record, stop condition, exception, repair, ownership boundary, or proof requirement. If a coverage-complete leaf exceeds it, keep it until testing identifies a non-inferior compression.

### Shared handoff without hidden hierarchy

Each leaf should emit the same compact record fields when relevant: artifact/location, concern, evidence class, authority and mandate, affected party, intended context, current state, condition/expiry, smallest fallback, owner, and proof. This is a schema-level handshake, not a runtime dependency. No new “ethics” expert should sit above them. Core continues to prohibit fabricated facts/claims and to preserve explicit unknowns; the directly selected leaf supplies domain stops and routing.

### Adoption-priority boundary

None of these risk leaves should own typography pairing, spacing scales, generic white space, or a universal “inclusive aesthetic.” Culture may require correct names, scripts, reading direction, image context, hierarchy of voices, or preservation of a map note, then route the design relationship to Typography/Composition. Rights may require a live disclosure or credit, then Typography/Composition determines its legible placement. Sustainability may require a qualification adjacent and prominent enough not to contradict the hero claim, then Brand/Composition/Type implement the relation. Visible hierarchy and subject-specific composition remain proof targets; fixed recipes remain rejected.

### Routing and regression checks

- “Check this public JPEG for GPS and participant metadata” selects Rights/Privacy/Provenance, not the cultural or sustainability leaf.
- “Develop a campaign with a supplied community protocol and approved motifs” selects Culture/Representation; Rights joins only when assets, people, privacy, synthetic evidence, or jurisdiction are material.
- “Critique whether this green package overstates a supplied recycled-content fact” selects Sustainability Claims plus the needed visual owner; it does not load cultural protocol or C2PA by default.
- “Map disputed boundaries using the publisher’s current policy” selects Culture plus Rights/Jurisdiction and Information/Data; it preserves actual map, note, and label decisions.
- “Use a valid Content Credential as proof the depicted event happened” selects Rights/Privacy/Provenance and must reject the truth inference.
- “Create an inclusive layout” does not select Culture merely because representation is a desirable value; a concrete culture/authority/identity risk must be material.
- Current strengths must not regress: authority mapping, evidence-class separation, participant agency, no invented heritage/endorsement/benefit, premise-level repair, C2PA boundary, named escalation, and no cultural-safety self-certification.

### Proof labels

Deterministic evidence can prove that required fields exist, protected strings and feature IDs survive, prohibited fields/assets are absent, manifests validate, routes select correctly, and public derivatives match the declared metadata policy. Render evidence can show the actual hierarchy, adjacency, crop, map note, disclosure, credit, and visual environmental implication. Neither proves cultural legitimacy, informed consent quality, legal clearance, factual truth, environmental benefit, or absence of downstream harm.

Human review must name its authority and scope. One consultant or reviewer cannot support population-wide acceptance. Real-world consultation can validate a specified use under specified conditions; it cannot prove universal cultural safety. Legal, privacy, safeguarding, documentary, geospatial, lifecycle, certification, and accessibility judgments remain with current competent owners.

### Claim ceiling

After a bounded successor intervention and successful open tests, Scoville Design may claim it can:

- identify and localize common authority, representation, consent, privacy, rights, provenance, documentary, map/name/symbol, and environmental-claim risks;
- preserve facts, unknowns, authority mandates, conditions, and dissent in a design decision record;
- distinguish documentary, constructed, illustrative, and synthetic roles;
- request field-level asset, consent/use, metadata, clearance, and claim evidence;
- block specified unresolved release states and propose the smallest non-fabricating fallback;
- route the consequential decision to the named community, participant, native-reader, legal/privacy/safeguarding, geospatial, documentary, rights, lifecycle, certification, accessibility, or production authority.

It must not claim culture-, country-, religion-, ethnicity-, community-, or Indigenous-specific mastery; universal inclusion or offense avoidance; cultural safety; community consent from generic knowledge; legal advice or global permission; publicity/privacy compliance; child-safeguarding approval; map neutrality or sovereignty authority; documentary truth; authorship/rights/identity from C2PA; anonymity from redaction alone; environmental truth or certification; sustainability expertise; or expert-equivalent rights, privacy, editorial, cartographic, lifecycle, accessibility, or consultation practice. Open fixtures and one reviewer also cannot support population perception, stakeholder consensus, market impact, or reduction in real-world harm.

## 10. Priority (P0-P3)

### P0 — current omission can cause rights/privacy harm or material false proof

- Add a fail-closed release gate for unresolved secret/sacred authority, vulnerable-person safety or use permission, disputed map/name/official-symbol decisions without publisher authority, consequential personal/location/device/prompt metadata, missing material rights layers, false documentary role, and unsupported express or implied environmental claims.

- Add layered asset clearance for people, artwork, architecture, products, marks, text/maps, site terms, distributor policy, and current jurisdiction; a stock/photo licence is not a universal clearance.
