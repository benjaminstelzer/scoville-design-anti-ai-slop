# Canonical curriculum ledger

**Status:** research ledger, 2026-09-02  
**Scope:** Latin-first professional graphic-design curriculum for the Scoville Design Skill; non-Latin coverage is limited to the ADR-0016 safety floor.  
**Method:** `canonical-curriculum-method.md`; explicit combined Scoville Research + bounded Scoville Brainstorm. This is a bounded evidence synthesis, not a systematic or exhaustive review.

## 0. Contract, evidence model, and architecture decision

This ledger records sources rather than importing their prose. Copyrighted works are **reference-only**. Only mechanisms, rule clusters, conventions, heuristics, exceptions, exercises, and diagnostic targets are extracted. A stable URL proves retrievability, not canonicality or truth.

### Evidence and reuse classes

| Class | Meaning | Permitted use here |
|---|---|---|
| `CURR` | Design-school syllabus, curriculum, or public course/lecture | Canonicality and teaching-sequence evidence; not rule truth by itself |
| `BOOK-REF` | Copyrighted book or commercial edition | Bibliographic reference and paraphrased synthesis only; no copied prose, figures, tables, or exercises |
| `OPEN-READ` | Publicly readable scholarship/manual whose adaptation rights are absent, restricted, or source-specific | Read, cite, and paraphrase; do not transplant wording or assets |
| `OER-ADAPT` | Open educational content with an explicit adaptation licence | Rules may be independently rewritten with attribution and compatible licence handling |
| `STD` | Standard, specification, or normative accessibility/production source | Carry exact obligation and scope; distinguish normative from informative text and check current version |
| `PRO-MANUAL` | Current professional or institutional manual/code | Practice and proof requirements within its domain; never universalise an organisation-specific style |
| `PD/CC0` | Public-domain or CC0 material | Reuse may be allowed, but trademarks, privacy, publicity, third-party assets, and endorsement remain separate |

### Canonicality test

A source is treated as canonical enough for this ledger when at least one of these holds and its limitations are stated: (1) recurrent independent syllabus adoption; (2) field-defining historical influence plus continued specialist publication; (3) current official standard or professional consensus document; (4) current specialist research synthesis; or (5) a mature, openly reviewed teaching resource. Canonical does **not** mean universally correct, current in every technical detail, culturally neutral, or suitable for direct runtime transcription.

### Bounded architecture alternatives

1. **Flat bibliography:** cheapest to maintain, but hides lane coverage and encourages indiscriminate loading.
2. **Ten lane ledgers plus a cross-lane rule matrix — selected:** preserves canonicality evidence, rights, limits, and selective routing while extracting shared mechanisms once.
3. **Rule-only curriculum graph:** strongest deduplication, but it would sever edition, licence, and bias evidence from the rules and make audits brittle.

The selected architecture is established rather than novel: lane-specific reading lists plus an explicit synthesis matrix. Its value here is progressive disclosure. Source records remain research-only; future runtime work should load only the leaf reference that owns the relevant medium and problem.

**Decision model, not recipe:** first assign functional roles and text conditions; then inspect repertoire, optical size, weight/width range, voice, texture, numerals, punctuation, fallback, licensing, and rendering. One family can provide sufficient contrast; several can be justified by role, language, data, or brand. Count is an outcome of necessary roles and coherence—not a preset maximum. Pairing is proven in actual content, sizes, widths, states, print conditions, and fallback, not by genre labels or a named-font list.

## 13. ADR-0016 non-Latin safety floor

No source in this ledger authorises broad non-Latin design competence. For every non-Latin or mixed-script task:

1. Detect language, script, writing direction, required repertoire, and materiality before design decisions.
2. Require a font and renderer that support shaping, marks, fallback, line breaking, and direction for the actual text.
3. Do **not** transfer Latin tracking, casing, italic, hyphenation, justification, word-spacing, line-break, or pairing habits without script-specific authority.
4. Render the actual language and flag missing glyphs, tofu, incorrect joining/mark placement, fallback metric drift, clipped marks, and direction/order failures.
5. When correctness or cultural effect is material, require review by a native reader and/or qualified script specialist.

Runtime claim ceiling: **Latin typographic depth with a multiscript detection, rendering, and escalation floor**—not multiscript art direction or expert non-Latin typography.

## 14. Rights, currency, bias, and software boundary

- `BOOK-REF` and `OPEN-READ` records supply concepts and comparison targets only. Do not copy prose, diagrams, tables, examples, screenshots, or exercise wording.
- Open licences apply only to the identified work and version. Check embedded images, fonts, logos, trademarks, sample files, and third-party excerpts separately.
- A public-domain government manual can still contain protected insignia, marks, privacy/publicity interests, and implied-endorsement risks.
- Accessibility standards define scoped minimums and tests; they do not determine taste, hierarchy, reading comfort, CVD robustness, or print outcomes outside scope.
- Historical European/US modernist works are overrepresented because they are heavily canonised and bibliographically stable. Treat their formal systems as mechanisms and history, not culturally neutral defaults.
- Latin/English evidence dominates. This is an intentional depth decision under ADR-0016, paired with the non-Latin safety floor rather than false generalisation.
- Adobe, LaTeX, R/ggplot, browser, and platform-specific sources are implementation priors. Runtime instructions must name the capability and proof, not require one proprietary tool unless the artifact contract does.
- Current web drafts, browser support, colour/HDR capabilities, design-token standards, C2PA/IPTC vocabularies, and print supplier specifications can change. Recheck before technical claims or implementation.
- “Classic,” “standard work,” and syllabus recurrence indicate influence and teaching value, not empirical validation. When research conflicts with received wisdom, state the disagreement and test in context.

## 16. Major gaps and claim ceiling

1. **No exhaustive curriculum census:** the school sample establishes independent recurrence, not global frequency or rank.
2. **Geographic and canon bias:** Western European and North American institutions/books dominate; Latin depth is intentional, but cultural/style claims require broader local sources.
3. **Weak open licensing among canonical books:** most standard works are closed. Runtime wording must be independently authored; open OER cannot fully replace expert classics.
4. **Empirical limits:** typography research is heterogeneous; strong universal claims about serif/sans, exact measure, font count, pairings, spacing, harmony, or “best” grids are unsupported.
5. **Fast-moving technology:** variable fonts, CSS drafts, wide gamut/HDR, tokens, provenance, AI metadata, PDF workflows, and browser support require version checks.
6. **Supplier and jurisdiction dependence:** print/package/wayfinding proof, licences, privacy, consent, sustainability, and professional duties depend on medium, supplier, place, and date.
7. **No non-Latin expert curriculum:** ADR-0016 safety checks and escalation are the ceiling.

**Defensible claim:** this ledger supports a professionally grounded, source-traceable Latin graphic-design curriculum that can generate, distinguish, critique, and repair typography, composition, colour, imagery, information, identity, responsive/fixed-medium, and production decisions while labelling conventions, evidence limits, exceptions, rights, and proof. It does not prove universal taste, exhaustive global canon coverage, autonomous production correctness, legal compliance, or expert multiscript design.
