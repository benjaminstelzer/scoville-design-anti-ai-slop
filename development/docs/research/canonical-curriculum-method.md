# Canonical books, lectures, and curriculum research method

Date: 2026-09-02  
Status: mandatory W-011 research lane

## Purpose

Identify the books, lectures, courses, syllabi, open educational resources,
professional manuals, and historical sources that repeatedly underpin serious
education in typography, layout, graphic design, colour, imagery, and design
critique. Use them to test coverage and derive original operational synthesis,
not to copy prose, diagrams, exercises, or proprietary examples.

## Source classes

For every item record:

- title, author/institution, edition or course date, stable identifier and URL;
- why it is treated as canonical, current, specialist, historical, or merely
  popular;
- exact topics relevant to one or more runtime references;
- source class:
  - openly licensed and adaptable;
  - public domain or CC0;
  - openly accessible but restricted adaptation;
  - copyrighted reference-only;
  - commercial preview or metadata only;
  - current standard, official documentation, or professional guidance;
- license/reuse/quotation boundary and embedded third-party exceptions;
- currency, cultural/geographic bias, software dependence, and known limits;
- rules, conventions, heuristics, exceptions, exercises, and diagnostic views
  the item can legitimately inform;
- whether the item adds necessary Skill instruction or only model-prior/history.

## Canonicality evidence

Do not call a work a standard merely because it is famous. Prefer evidence such
as repeated use across independent design-school syllabi, professional
bibliographies, later editions, publisher/institution records, or sustained
specialist citation. Record when this evidence is incomplete.

Course and syllabus recurrence is coverage evidence, not proof that every
teaching claim is universally correct. Practitioner books supply established
craft conventions and vocabulary; empirical research and standards still
govern claims about perception, accessibility, technology, or safety.

## Required subject lanes

1. Latin typography and typesetting;
2. typeface selection, pairing, family count, and type systems;
3. kerning, tracking, word spacing, leading, measure, paragraphs, microtype,
   page and column breaks, numerals, optical sizes, fallback, and proof;
4. composition, grids, hierarchy, spacing, rhythm, negative space, tension,
   asymmetry, crop, editorial spreads, and responsive recomposition;
5. colour systems, perception, accessibility, and reproduction;
6. photography, illustration, art direction, selection, and sequencing;
7. information design, diagrams, data visualization, maps, and tables;
8. visual identity and design systems;
9. responsive web design as design intent and fixed print design as physical
   medium intent;
10. critique, iteration, presentation, production, ethics, and professional
    practice.

Non-Latin writing systems receive the ADR-0016 safety-floor search only unless
a source is necessary to prevent Latin-default misuse.

## Rule extraction

For every recurring rule cluster, record:

- exact design problem;
- source convergence and disagreement;
- rule strength: binding, evidence-bounded, convention, heuristic, preference,
  or deliberate exception;
- formal variables and observable failure signatures;
- smallest generation or repair move;
- context and medium;
- what evidence overrides the starting rule;
- verification method;
- target runtime reference and whether it belongs in Core, a leaf, a source
  map, an evaluation fixture, or nowhere in the runtime package.
