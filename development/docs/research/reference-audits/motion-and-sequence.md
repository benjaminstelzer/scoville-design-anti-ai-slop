## 1. Current contract

### Activation, ownership, maturity, and size

The Direct Index activates this leaf for **motion, timing, storyboard, and reduced mode**. `modules.yaml` routes `motion` and `sequence` to one `retained-floor` / `external-verification` module that owns `motion_thesis`, `temporal_hierarchy`, `continuity_spine`, and `reduced_equivalent`. The executable is roughly 300 prose words, far below the 1,800-token cost target.

Its sources are L-19, L-25, L-26, and L-27. L-19 is a competency map; L-25 and L-27 are noncommercial ShareAlike learning references; L-26 covers scoped Web reduced-motion behavior. The current rule map does not ground the explicit WCAG 2.2.2 pause/stop/hide or 2.3.1 flashing floors even though the leaf asks for loop and flashing-risk diagnosis. That source/rule gap is material.

`SOL-B10` is the only direct model evidence. All three open baseline arms produced coherent six-frame data-to-title storyboards plus static/reduced alternatives. This proves neither a specialist advantage nor reliable long-tail performance. It supports keeping a functional safety/verification floor and argues against broad lessons on animation principles, style, easing recipes, or software.

### Existing generation contract

The leaf already asks for purpose, duration, format, audience, beats, assets, persistent elements, trigger, audio, loop/end state, platform/export, performance, flashing risk, and reduced/static requirements. Generation requires:

- one motion thesis: what changes, why, and what stays fixed;
- entry, development, transition, resolution, and hold;
- a continuity spine such as datum, object, contour, baseline, route, camera relation, or sound cue;
- timing ranges, easing character, synchronization, and exit conditions in medium-appropriate language;
- reduced/static design from the same essential information.

### Existing critique, repair, exception, and verification contract

Critique asks whether motion reveals causality, hierarchy, change, navigation, or feedback rather than spectacle. It checks reading holds, competing movement, continuity breaks, accidental loops, layout shift, input interruption, flashing, vestibular risk, and reduced-mode meaning. Repair targets timing or the transformation rule before polish. Abrupt cuts, jitter, stroboscopic texture, nonphysical easing, and deliberate discontinuity may be intentional only inside safety, platform, and meaning constraints. Verification requires storyboard, timed preview, final hold, loop point, reduced/static version, and target platform.

### Current ownership boundary

Design Motion owns the **temporal communication decision**: thesis, hierarchy, beats, continuity, pacing, easing character, loop/end state, kinetic type behavior, reduced/static equivalent, and visual judgment. It may state implementation constraints, but it does not own:

- framework/component APIs, DOM/focus/state implementation, preference detection, input behavior, or browser/device performance; these belong to Scoville UI and Code when active;
- codec/container/platform delivery, source collection, render queue, captions package, production preflight, or vendor approval; these belong to Media Production and Handoff;
- data truth, audio engineering, localization, or legal/platform claims without their respective owner and evidence.

When Design and UI both apply, the Core’s single compact record should hand off the intended temporal relation and validation target. Motion does not become the UI implementer, and UI does not silently redesign the motion thesis.

## 2. What is already strong

1. **Change plus invariant.** “What changes, why, and what remains fixed” is a compact causal model that works for filmic sequences, kinetic typography, data animation, logos, and interaction transitions.
2. **Temporal hierarchy is owned explicitly.** The module treats time as a hierarchy and communication dimension, not merely a set of keyframes.
3. **Beat structure includes a hold.** Entry/development/transition/resolution/hold prevents the common mistake of animating continuously without a readable state.
4. **Continuity spine is concrete.** Datum, object, contour, baseline, route, camera relation, and sound cue are transferable continuity mechanisms rather than style recipes.
5. **Timing is expressed as character and range.** The leaf avoids one universal duration or easing curve and asks for production language appropriate to the medium.
6. **Reduced/static equivalence starts at generation.** This is stronger than adding `prefers-reduced-motion` after the primary design is complete.
7. **Critique is functional and causal.** It separates motion that communicates causality, hierarchy, change, navigation, or feedback from spectacle.
8. **Interruption and layout shift are visible.** These are common UI implementation failures that pure animation teaching misses.
9. **Exceptions are genuinely breakable.** Abrupt cuts, jitter, nonphysical easing, and discontinuity can be correct when their communicative purpose and compensation are explicit.
10. **Claim ceiling is honest.** The module does not equate a storyboard with production animation, a preview with platform performance, or a file with delivery approval.

The intervention should preserve this conceptual economy. Professional depth comes from better temporal diagnosis, exact safety scope, and proof—not from importing the “12 principles,” fixed duration ladders, frame-rate slogans, easing token libraries, or After Effects tutorials.

## 3. Missing professional capability

### Temporal hierarchy and motion density

The current leaf names temporal hierarchy but does not operationalize it. A professional spec should identify:

- the primary change, secondary support, and elements that must remain quiet or fixed;
- when attention enters, transfers, rests, and exits;
- simultaneous versus sequential actions and which event is causal;
- visual, typographic, audio, and interaction events competing for the same attention window;
- the stable state before and after the motion and whether the transition changes meaning, ownership, or hierarchy.

Without this, “stagger” becomes decoration and several individually polished movements flatten the intended priority.

### Continuity, interruption, and reversibility

Continuity is more than a shared element. It can be spatial (where an object comes from), semantic (same role across states), causal (action produces result), temporal (order and duration), or perceptual (shape/colour/trajectory link). Interaction motion must specify what happens when input reverses, repeats, arrives mid-transition, the target disappears, data changes, the user navigates away, the API is unavailable, or rendering drops frames. The interface state must remain correct even if the animation is skipped or cancelled.

### Pacing, reading holds, and hierarchy over time

The leaf needs a hold/readability decision, not a fixed number. Hold length depends on text amount, language/script, type size, contrast, reading task, audience, narration/captions, screen size, viewing distance, and whether the viewer controls playback. A sequence can be slow yet unreadable if information appears before attention arrives, or fast yet clear when one stable state carries the complete message.

Pacing should distinguish active movement from rest, transition from comprehension, and duration from rhythm. Every beat needs an entry condition, readable state, exit condition, and reason for its relative duration.

### Easing and path as meaning

Easing should be specified by velocity/acceleration character and semantic role: enter and settle; depart; transfer; continuous progress; elastic confirmation; interrupted or gesture-tracked motion. “Natural,” “smooth,” “physics-based,” “ease-out,” and “exit faster than entry” are not universal rules. Linear progression can be correct for time/data; abrupt or stepped motion can communicate a cut, machine state, alarm, or stylistic discontinuity. The rule is to match the change and interaction, avoid accidental velocity discontinuity, and test the perceived consequence.

### Loops and end states

The current loop check needs a complete contract: loop purpose; finite or indefinite repetition; first, final, poster, and seam frames; whether the loop resets state or implies impossible causality; what pauses or persists; whether repeated attention becomes distraction; how playback is stopped; and what reduced/static mode shows. An ambient loop, progress indicator, instructional cycle, data playback, and decorative animation require different controls and truth conditions.

### Kinetic typography and localization

Kinetic type needs an explicit **reveal → stable read → transformation → stable result** model. Professional variables include line/word/glyph unit, reading direction, script shaping, word segmentation, glyph integrity, hierarchy, spatial path, deformation, motion blur, overlap, contrast, audio synchronization, caption collision, language expansion, and user-controlled replay/pause. Motion may add emotion or implicative meaning, but research shows the effect depends on the combination of word and motion; it is not a universal emotion dictionary.

Legibility must be checked for the actual script and viewers. A Hangul study found moving-character reading sensitive to character complexity, size, and presentation time; this cannot be converted into Latin defaults or fixed hold durations.

### Reduced and static equivalence

Reduced motion is not “remove all animation,” “replace everything with opacity,” or “hide the content.” The target is to remove or replace non-essential movement that can cause discomfort or distraction while preserving state, order, causality, progress, feedback, and completion. Large spatial travel, scale/zoom, parallax, depth/camera movement, rotation, bounce, peripheral motion, and repeated oscillation deserve particular scrutiny, but the appropriate replacement is task- and platform-specific.

The static equivalent must communicate all essential information and relationships, not merely show the first frame. It may require a final composite, labelled key states, diagram, transcript/caption, or step sequence. A reduced interaction may retain immediate colour/shape/state change when that is the clearest feedback.

### WCAG scope, vestibular risk, and flashing

Three distinct WCAG 2.2 requirements must not be collapsed:

- **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A):** automatically starting moving/blinking/scrolling information lasting more than five seconds and presented alongside other content needs pause/stop/hide unless essential; auto-updating content has a related control/frequency rule without the five-second condition.
- **2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold (Level A):** no content may flash more than three times in one second unless it is below the general/red flash thresholds. A warning or late off switch is not a substitute; seizure harm can occur before action.
- **2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA):** interaction-triggered motion animation can be disabled unless essential. It is AAA, not a universal AA conformance requirement, though reduced-motion support remains strong inclusive practice and may be required by platform/product policy.

Vestibular risk is broader than flashes: parallax, large-field motion, zoom/scale, camera/depth movement, multi-axis travel, rotation, bounce, blur transitions, and peripheral movement can provoke dizziness, nausea, migraine, or loss of function. The leaf needs a risk screen and a real reduced-mode inspection, not diagnosis of a user’s medical condition.

### Interaction motion, Design versus UI

For interface work, Design should specify start/end states, preserved context, spatial/semantic relation, hierarchy, timing/easing character, interrupt/reverse behavior, reduced equivalent, and expected evidence. UI should map that specification to incumbent component and platform patterns, focus/DOM/state semantics, input mechanisms, `prefers-reduced-motion` or native settings, cancellation, unsupported APIs, and actual responsive/performance testing. If the user requests only implementation of a settled motion system, Motion need not load.

### Performance, export, and handoff

“Performance” needs observable criteria: response to input is not delayed; no content/state depends on animation completion; playback remains acceptable on representative low- and high-capability targets; layout, paint, compositing, memory, battery, download, long tasks, and simultaneous animations are profiled as applicable; skipped/dropped frames do not corrupt meaning. Transform/opacity and 60 fps are useful Web heuristics, not proof or universal laws.

The handoff must name: editable source; dimensions/aspects; duration/time base; frame rate and frame count; first/final/poster/loop frames; safe areas; colour/bit depth; transparency/alpha; audio role, sample settings, and sync reference; captions/transcript; fonts/plugins/assets and licences; output format/container/codec/compression; platform limits; state/marker names; responsive variants; reduced/static files; filenames/versions; render settings/log; and validation status. Only destination documentation and an actual encoded/runtime test can establish delivery.

## 4. Rule-quality audit

| Rule or claim | Class | Audit judgment | Professional replacement or exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every movement has a communication job | Strong evidence-bounded rule | Keep. It supports hierarchy and removal decisions without dictating style. | Ambient or material motion may carry tone rather than discrete information, but its intended effect, distraction budget, loop control, and reduced equivalent must be named. |
| Keep one continuity spine | Strong heuristic | Keep as a starting point, not a count law. | Multiple spines may be necessary for parallel narratives; deliberate discontinuity may reject continuity when the break itself communicates. |
| Entry, development, transition, resolution, hold | Useful beat model | Keep but allow different structures. | Loops, gesture-tracked transitions, live data, and non-linear experiences may lack a single entry/resolution; they still need readable states and stop/fallback behavior. |
| Motion clarifies, never decorates | Slogan/overstatement | Replace. Motion can create tone, pleasure, materiality, or identity without explaining state. | Decorative motion remains valid when it does not impair task, safety, access, performance, or hierarchy and has a reduced/stop strategy where required. |
| Motion should be invisible | Preference/slogan | Reject. Kinetic type, title sequences, alerts, and expressive transitions may intentionally foreground motion. | Judge whether attention serves the thesis and whether essential task/state remains clear. |
| Use ease-out; exits are faster; never use ease-in | Contextual UI heuristic | Reject as universal. | Specify semantic velocity and test perceived response; gesture tracking, continuous data, mechanical cuts, and narrative motion need different timing functions. |
| Motion under 100 ms is instant; 100–300 responsive; over 500 sluggish | Cargo-cult numeric range | Reject outside a tested system. | Duration follows distance/scale, information, input, platform, audience, and existing design-system tokens; measure task response and readability. |
| 60 fps or do not animate / at least 24 fps | Unsupported universal | Reject. Refresh rates, media cadence, stop-motion aesthetics, power/device constraints, and delivery formats differ. | Choose a time base/frame rate intentionally, then test temporal accuracy, smoothness/style, safety, and performance on target outputs. |
| Animate only transform and opacity | Web performance heuristic | Keep as a candidate implementation optimization, not a Design rule. | Layout/shape/colour/clip/path motion may be necessary; profile actual implementation and preserve semantics. Avoid blanket GPU hacks. |
| Smooth/physical motion is more professional | Preference | Reject. | Physicality is one language. Linear, stepped, abrupt, jittered, or nonphysical motion can be correct if intentional, safe, and semantically coherent. |
| Stroboscopic texture may be a style exception | Dangerous unless tightly bounded | Keep only with a non-waivable safety floor. | No aesthetic rationale overrides WCAG 2.3.1 or the target platform/broadcast standard. Run an appropriate flash analysis or remove/slow/reduce the area. |
| More than three flashes always fails 2.3.1 | Incomplete normative shorthand | Correct the scope. | It may satisfy 2.3.1 only if below the defined general/red flash thresholds; use exact criterion/tool. Level AAA 2.3.2 is stricter. |
| Any animation over five seconds needs pause | Incorrect shorthand | Correct the scope. | WCAG 2.2.2 also requires automatic start and parallel presentation; auto-updating has different conditions. Product policy may intentionally be stricter. |
| Interaction motion must be disabled under WCAG AA | False conformance claim | Reject. 2.3.3 is Level AAA. | Preserve AAA scope while treating reduction as inclusive practice or a separate platform/product requirement. |
| `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` means no motion | False implementation rule | Reject. | Remove or replace non-essential harmful motion; preserve essential information, feedback, order, and state with the least motion necessary. |
| Static equivalent is the first or last frame | False equivalence | Reject. | Provide all essential states/relationships/information, potentially as a labelled sequence, composite, diagram, caption/transcript, or alternate interaction. |
| Kinetic type may be read while moving | Risky assumption | Replace. | Provide stable readable states and test actual script, text complexity, duration, screen, audience, audio/captions, and localization. Expressive deformation must not destroy required recognition. |
| Repeated transitions use named roles/tokens | Strong system heuristic | Keep. | Token consistency must not force identical distance, duration, or motion when scale, context, or state relation differs. |
| A storyboard validates motion | False proof claim | Reject. | Storyboard validates beats and content; timed animatic, target render/runtime, reduced/static path, safety analysis, and performance/export tests validate different claims. |

Every consequential critique should be expressed as **visible temporal failure → likely cause in hierarchy/timing/transformation/state → user or narrative consequence → smallest coherent repair → exact timed/runtime/production evidence**.

### Evidence synthesis and contradictions

- **Safety versus style:** the current leaf correctly allows jitter and stroboscopic texture only inside safety constraints. WCAG 2.3.1 establishes a non-waivable Level A boundary; IBM, Skill templates, and style references cannot override it.
- **Reduced motion:** W3C defines reduction around non-essential harmful/distraction motion, while Apple illustrates practical replacements and platform behavior. Neither supports deleting essential feedback or showing an informationally incomplete static frame.
- **Easing and duration:** Carbon, Apple, and public Skills offer useful system-specific tokens and patterns but disagree in curves, bands, and physicality. The stable rule is semantic role plus actual system/target testing, not the number.
- **Frame rate/performance:** IBM says at least 24 fps; Web guidance often targets 60 fps; stylized animation, displays, broadcast, native UI, power states, and codecs differ. Exact cadence is a destination decision. Performance is measured, not inferred from a slogan.
- **Kinetic type:** motion can add emotional/semantic meaning, but its interaction with word, script, size, complexity, and exposure time is bounded. There is no universal “anger bounce” or fixed reading hold.

[L-25, M-A6] |

[M-A7] |

[M-A2] |

[M-A7; Core ownership] |
| Performance | Choose the lightest mechanism; avoid gating input; profile real layout/paint/compositing/decode/memory/network behavior on representative targets. | Promise 60 fps, force GPU layers, or approve transform/opacity from source inspection alone. | “Scroll-linked animation is smooth on desktop but janks input on low-end mobile” → main-thread/decode cost under target load → simplify/disable/offload as measured → performance trace and task response on target. [M-A9] |

[M-A10, M-A11] |

## 8. Architecture recommendation

### Bounded mechanism comparison

1. **Retain one professional motion leaf and deepen it:** shared temporal contract spanning motion graphics, kinetic type, storyboard, data motion, interaction-motion judgment, safety, reduced/static equivalence, and production evidence—while preserving external implementation owners.
2. **Split into `motion-graphics-and-storyboards` and `interaction-motion`:** each could carry deeper medium syntax, but many tasks cross the boundary and UI already owns implementation. The router has no proven independent signal for two new Design leaves beyond `motion`/`sequence`.
3. **Keep only a safety/accessibility stub:** rely on model knowledge for craft and load UI/Media for implementation. This is token-cheap but loses causal temporal diagnosis, kinetic readability, continuity, and non-UI motion judgment.

### Recommendation: retain one leaf; professional coverage outranks the 1,800-token target

Mechanism 1 is the correct architecture. Motion hierarchy, continuity, pacing, holds, easing, loops, kinetic type, reduced/static equivalence, and safety share one temporal decision model. Splitting by output medium would duplicate these rules and create mixed-task ambiguity. Interaction implementation remains separately owned by UI/Code, so the larger leaf does not need to become an API tutorial.

The 1,800-token figure is a **cost target, not a correctness ceiling**. Draft the complete professional floor first, validate it, then compress repeated explanation. If exact WCAG scope, kinetic readability, ownership, or production evidence cannot fit without ambiguity, retain a larger selectively routed leaf. A split is justified only after open routing evidence shows stable independent signals and reduced total context—not merely because the leaf exceeds a number.

The future executable should be organized around seven gates:

1. **Frame and mode:** communication job, linear/looped/interactive/live mode, audience, platform, duration, assets/data/audio, safety/access requirements.
2. **Temporal model:** motion thesis, stable states, beats, primary/secondary/quiet hierarchy, attention and reading holds.
3. **Transformation:** continuity type/spine, path, easing character, synchronization, entry/exit, interruption/reverse/cancel, final/loop state.
4. **Type and meaning:** kinetic unit/script/localization, glyph integrity, captions, data truth, and content preservation.
5. **Reduced/static:** essential information and state equivalence, user control, preference/platform behavior, vestibular risk.
6. **Safety and owner handoff:** exact WCAG 2.2.2/2.3.1/2.3.3 scope; Design specification versus UI/Media/Code implementation and verification.
7. **Proof:** storyboard/animatic, timed target render/runtime, slow/key-frame inspection, full loop, reduced/static, flash analysis, performance trace, encoded/imported delivery.

### Routing corrections and progressive disclosure

- “Motion,” “timing,” “kinetic type,” “storyboard,” “temporal pacing/continuity,” or “reduced/static motion” selects Motion.
- A **static image sequence** selects Imagery; a **temporal sequence** selects Motion. Bare “sequence” should be disambiguated by the task rather than automatically loading both.
- Settled UI-motion implementation selects UI/Code only. New interaction-motion definition or visual judgment plus implementation selects Motion + UI, using the Core record.
- Video/Lottie/export/preflight alone selects Media Production; Motion joins when temporal design or critique is material.
- Data animation selects Information/Data for encoding/truth and Motion for temporal hierarchy only when both are independently material.

The reference should not follow these owners into their implementation files. It records the motion decision and unresolved evidence once.

### Architecture and routing tests

- `Create a six-frame motion storyboard with kinetic type` selects Motion, not automatic Imagery.
- `Select and order six still campaign photographs` selects Imagery, not Motion.
- `Implement the already approved framework transition tokens` selects UI/Code; Motion remains unloaded unless design judgment changes.
- `Design and implement a new route transition preserving list context` selects Motion + UI and uses one handoff record.
- `Export this approved animation to broadcast, Web, and Lottie` selects Media Production; Motion joins only for temporal/reduced/visual validation.
- `Animate verified chart data over time` selects Data + Motion; Motion may not change values or imply unsupported causality.
- A larger leaf must still load only on these direct signals; compression or splitting is evaluated by total routed tokens and outcome, not leaf size alone.

### Claim ceiling

After the recommended intervention, the Skill may claim that it can frame, generate, distinguish, critique, and repair temporal design; define hierarchy, beats, holds, continuity, pacing, easing character, loops, kinetic type, reduced/static equivalents, safety checks, and implementation/production evidence requirements; and preserve Design/UI/Media ownership.

It must not claim professional animator-, motion-director-, kinetic-type-, storyboard-, audio-, accessibility-medical-, performance-engineer-, codec-, broadcast-, or platform-delivery equivalence; universal timing/easing/frame-rate rules; WCAG conformance without target testing; vestibular safety from `prefers-reduced-motion` alone; flash safety from visual inspection or warning; readable kinetic type without script/audience testing; runtime performance from property choice; or production readiness from a storyboard, source file, local preview, or single encode. One open storyboard and one reviewer cannot establish specialist improvement, market preference, or cross-platform reliability.

## 10. Priority (P0-P3)

### P0 — current guidance can cause safety, access, truth, or ownership failure

- Add exact WCAG 2.2.2 Level A scope for qualifying automatic movement/updates and controls.
- Add exact WCAG 2.3.1 Level A flash boundary, final-variant analysis, and the rule that warning/off-switch/style cannot compensate for unsafe flashes.
- State that WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions is Level AAA; do not mislabel it as universal AA while still requiring honest reduced behavior when platform/product scope demands it.
- Define reduced/static **information and state equivalence**, not simple deletion/fade/poster frame.
- Add interruption/cancel/reverse and canonical-state rules so animation cannot corrupt or block UI behavior.
- Preserve Design versus UI/Code/Media ownership in the motion record and proof labels.

### P1 — missing applied capability blocks the all-round designer goal

- Operationalize temporal hierarchy, attention transfer, motion density, stable states, and causal order.
- Add content-dependent pacing/reading holds and kinetic-type script/localization diagnostics.
- Add semantic easing/path, continuity categories, loop/end/seam/control, and deliberate discontinuity repair.
- Add performance and complete export/handoff evidence fields without teaching a codec or framework.
- Add failure→cause→repair→timed proof structure for generate, critique, repair, and exceptions.
- Clarify static image sequence versus temporal sequence routing.

### P2 — useful depth and efficiency

- Add a vestibular-risk screen for large-field, peripheral, parallax, zoom/depth/camera, multi-axis, rotation, bounce, blur, and repeated motion, with platform-specific replacement tests.
- Add exact-frame/slow-play inspection, two-cycle loop inspection, and key-state capture to ordinary verification.
- Add audit-local source candidates for kinetic-type research, current platform motion guidance, performance, export, and runtime handoff to a later ledger/rule-map update.
- Measure the full professional draft before compression; accept a leaf over 1,800 tokens if lossless compression cannot preserve safety and ownership.

### P3 — defer or reject

- Do not add the 12 animation principles as a general curriculum, emotion/easing dictionaries, fixed duration/stagger/hold bands, “exit faster,” “never ease-in,” 24/60-fps floors, or transform/opacity-only rules.
- Do not add effect/glitch/logo/kinetic templates, stock motion tokens, plugin/library recommendations, camera recipes, or named-studio imitation.
- Do not split narrative and interaction motion until real routing/evaluation evidence shows stable independent signals and lower total context.
- Do not teach framework APIs, Lottie/dotLottie schemas, After Effects operation, codecs, broadcast standards, audio engineering, or medical guidance inside the Design leaf. Route current implementation and delivery to the actual owner.

**Overall audit result:** retain one selectively routed motion leaf and deepen it to professional temporal-decision depth. Correct safety scope, readable states, continuity under interruption, reduced/static equivalence, and target evidence matter more than the 1,800-token cost target. Compression follows coverage and validation; it must not erase the reasons the retained floor exists.
