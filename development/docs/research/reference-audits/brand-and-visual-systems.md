# Reference audit: `brand-and-visual-systems`

Date: 2026-09-02

Method: [Per-reference expert-depth audit method](../reference-audit-method.md)

Research mode: Scoville Research, mixed Development and Academic route, with a
bounded single-coordinator Brainstorm pass

Freshness boundary: public sources and repositories inspected through
2026-09-02

## Research contract and conclusion

This audit asks what the Brand leaf must contribute beyond Terra High's prior
knowledge so that visual identity, campaign family, template system, and
governance work is source-bounded, testable, and safe across touchpoints. It
includes identity-system principles, invariant and variable elements,
recognition evidence, mark variants and reproduction, campaign exceptions,
governance/versioning, accessibility, assets/IP/trademark escalation, and the
boundary to product UI systems. It excludes doing brand strategy, naming,
architecture, legal clearance, or specialist mark design by implication.

**Conclusion:** retain one directly routed Brand leaf, but deepen it into a
professionally complete identity-lifecycle and external-verification compiler.
Keep its maturity status `stub` until behavior evidence supports promotion; do
not treat that status or the 1,800-token cost target as a coverage ceiling. The
current 437-token stub already has the right system abstraction—visual thesis,
invariants, controlled variation, touchpoint tests, and governance—but it lacks
a reliable status model for proposed versus approved assets, a production
matrix for mark variants, recognition claim limits, campaign expiry,
source-of-truth/version controls, and a crisp boundary between brand identity
tokens and product UI semantics. These concerns are causally coupled across the
same asset lifecycle, so omitting them to hit a token target would weaken
correctness. They are `P1` gaps. No public Skill found in the bounded search
combines cross-touchpoint identity, consumer-recognition evidence,
rights/clearance escalation, production verification, and governance without
universal recipes or ownership inflation.

The bounded Brainstorm considered three architecture mechanisms in section 8.
It was one coordinated pass with no claim of isolated or independent
generation.

The direct route is well scoped: an isolated artifact with no system
consequence does not activate the module. The Brand leaf owns what makes a
visual family identifiable and governable; other leaves own the actual
typography, colour/reproduction, imagery, composition, UI workflow, production,
rights, and attribution concern when each is independently material.

There is one evidence-trace inconsistency to resolve later. The module manifest
lists `SOL-B01`, but the rendered-findings table defines SOL-B01 as the smoke
poster, while the Brand reference and admission ledger describe the evidence as
an imagery campaign/system case. Neither is an identity-system generation,
critique, repair, rollout, or governance test. The audit therefore treats the
Brand domain as having **no brand-specific qualified behavior evidence**.

### Current operational rules

**Generation**

- Record verified organization/product meaning, audiences, contexts, existing
  assets and owners, names and claims, category/competitor constraints,
  touchpoints, production realities, accessibility, legal/rights review, and
  approval authority.
- Mark research gaps and do not invent strategy.
- Write a visual thesis and a small set of principles tied to meaning.
- Separate invariants from controlled variation across mark, type, colour,
  composition, imagery, motion, voice interface, and production as applicable.
- Test materially different touchpoints and difficult content, not only a hero
  mockup.
- Define exceptions, approval, sources, versioning, and local adaptability.

**Critique**

- Check recognition without the logo, category/subject specificity,
  consistency versus dead uniformity, accessibility, edge cases, asset sprawl,
  unsupported claims, and whether governance is usable.

**Repair**

- Repair a principle or token that causes repeated failures instead of patching
  each artifact independently.

**Exceptions**

- A campaign may bend identity conventions when the relationship remains
  legible and the exception is documented.
- A mood board, logo sheet, or visual trend is not a complete brand system.

**Verification and claim boundary**

- Render representative touchpoints and record unresolved approvals.
- Treat L-21 as one open system case and L-22 as introductory visual-identity
  material, not universal process authority.
- Do not claim naming, positioning, brand architecture, trademark clearance,
  stakeholder alignment, or specialist mark design.

### Current evaluation boundary

The SOL baseline's fictional bakery case demonstrated that neutral, Core-only,
and generic-checklist arms could already create extensive art-direction systems
with sequence, locality, consent, rights, crops, anti-cliche controls, and
production questions. It did not ask for or prove a complete identity system,
mark family, consumer recognition, system governance, production rollout, or
repair. The generic checklist supplied no repeatable advantage.

Terra High has not loaded or tested this Brand leaf. One poster repair preserved
an existing black, cream, and signal-red identity, but that task tested
composition, typography, colour, source validity, and render fit—not identity
definition or governance. No current Terra evidence supports a brand capability
claim. The maturity ledger correctly leaves the domain `source-grounded` and
requires identity research, mark/system alternatives, architecture, rollout,
governance, production, and stakeholder validation before advancing.

## 2. What is already strong

1. **Activation is consequence-based.** Requiring a system consequence avoids
   loading brand logic merely because one artifact has colours or a logo.
2. **Strategy is input, not invented output.** Verified meaning, audiences,
   names, claims, category constraints, and owners are recorded before visual
   work. This is the most important defense against a design artifact silently
   becoming positioning, naming, or claim authority.
3. **Authority is part of the brief.** Existing assets, asset owners, approval
   authority, legal/rights review, and open approvals make the system
   organizational rather than purely aesthetic.
4. **The visual thesis precedes the asset list.** A small set of principles tied
   to meaning is more causal than a palette-and-font inventory.
5. **Invariants and controlled variation are explicitly separated.** This is
   the central mechanism of a visual system: identity does not require every
   touchpoint to look identical, but variation must preserve a recognizable
   relationship.
6. **The module spans the real identity surface.** Mark, type, colour,
   composition, imagery, motion, voice interface, and production constraints
   are considered conditionally instead of assuming “brand equals logo.”
7. **Touchpoint tests are materially different.** Difficult content and diverse
   applications can falsify a system that looks convincing only in a hero
   mockup.
8. **Critique includes both drift and over-control.** “Consistency versus dead
   uniformity,” asset sprawl, category specificity, accessibility, unsupported
   claims, and governability are stronger than a compliance-only checklist.
9. **Repair is causal.** Fixing the system principle or token that creates
   repeated failures is the correct parent-cause abstraction.
10. **Campaign exceptions are bounded.** The temporary expression must remain
    related and documented rather than becoming an undeclared rebrand.
11. **The claim ceiling is unusually honest.** Naming, positioning,
    architecture, trademark clearance, stakeholder alignment, and specialist
    mark design are explicitly excluded.

These mechanisms should remain recognizable in any later revision. Compress
only after professional coverage and routing correctness are demonstrated. The
audit does not justify a brand-theory essay, archetype catalogue, fixed
deliverable list, or universal logo recipe.

## 3. Missing professional capability

### Evidence state and authority

- The leaf does not distinguish **supplied fact**, **observed asset**,
  **proposed identity rule**, **approved rule**, **released asset**,
  **deprecated asset**, **rights-cleared asset**, and **registered trademark**.
  A generated guideline can otherwise make a proposal look official.
- Each consequential item needs an owner, source, status, scope, version, and
  approval/evidence state. “Existing logo” is insufficient if three conflicting
  files exist and nobody knows which is canonical.
- Missing strategy should remain a named blocker or assumption. It must not be
  backfilled with invented mission, values, personality, positioning, audience,
  proof points, or brand architecture.

### Identity architecture: invariants, variables, and element priority

- The leaf lists possible invariant domains but gives no method to decide which
  elements actually carry identity. It needs a compact hierarchy:
  **primary identifiers**, **signature cues**, **supporting rules**, and
  **channel/campaign variables**.
- “Invariant” must not mean visually frozen. The agent should record the
  element's semantic job, allowed transformations, prohibited transformations,
  and fallback behavior.
- Flexible or dynamic identities need a grammar: what may vary, through which
  operator, within which range/context, while which anchor remains stable.
  Fixed-versus-variable is a system decision, not a stylistic preference.
- A system should be tested both with and without the primary mark, but the
  result is a design judgment unless observed users can identify the brand.

### Recognition and distinctiveness claim ceiling

- “Recognition without the logo” is currently phrased as a check but can be
  misreported as fact. Recent multi-country/category research found that brand
  managers often misestimate how famous and unique brand elements are in
  consumer memory; group judgment improved estimates, but consumer data remains
  the relevant evidence (A-BV-04).
- Recognition is learned through exposure and association, not guaranteed by a
  distinctive-looking asset. Consistency can support learning, but repeated
  presentation does not prove notice, attribution, recall, or uniqueness.
- Logo complexity, naturalness, harmony, simplicity, or silhouette cannot be
  turned into universal recognition laws. Studies use different goals,
  exposure levels, populations, categories, and measures (A-BV-05, A-BV-06).
- The leaf needs separate claims: **visually differentiated in the audited
  set**, **reviewer can identify the family**, **target participants recognize
  the brand**, and **legal mark distinctiveness/clearance**. These are not
  interchangeable.

### Mark and lockup system

- The leaf names mark use but lacks a mark inventory: primary mark, wordmark,
  symbol/monogram where approved, horizontal/stacked lockups, positive,
  reversed, one-colour, small-context/favicons, co-brand/endorsement lockups,
  and retired variants only when each is needed.
- It should not assume every identity needs all of these variants. Derive the
  matrix from real touchpoints and production methods.
- Minimum size, clear space, stroke/detail survival, reverse behavior, and
  colour limits are mark-specific test results. Wikimedia's values are correct
  only for Wikimedia marks; Google's favicon dimensions are correct only for
  Google Search presentation (A-BV-07, A-BV-08).
- Small contexts may need a responsive mark rather than a scaled primary. A
  favicon, app icon, social avatar, embroidery, stamp, vinyl cut, one-colour
  print, reversed sign, and large-distance mark impose different constraints.
- Specialist mark construction/redesign remains outside this stub. When a
  supplied mark fails required contexts, report the failure and propose the
  required variant or redesign brief; do not silently redraw it.

### Reproduction and asset integrity

- The current leaf lacks canonical master format, colour-space/profile,
  transparency/background, view box/artboard, linked-font versus outlined-type,
  raster resolution, export size, file naming, checksum, and license/owner
  fields. These belong in the handoff when material, even if the production leaf
  performs the export.
- “One-colour” and “reversed” must be rendered from approved assets, not
  simulated by arbitrary filters. Print colour values and spot colours require
  actual supplier/production evidence.
- Favicon and platform icon requirements are volatile and channel-specific;
  verify the current platform contract rather than recalling a generic pack.

### Touchpoint and content stress tests

- “Materially different touchpoints” needs a selection method. Cover the
  smallest/highest-detail context, a one-colour or constrained production
  context, a large/distance context, a content-dense context, a responsive or
  localized context, and any co-brand/partner context actually in scope.
- Use real names, claims, languages, data, product imagery, legal lines,
  accessibility states, and missing/long content. A polished empty mockup does
  not test a system.
- Test recognition cues at family level: remove the logo only as a diagnostic,
  compare with category competitors, and ask whether the surviving combination
  is specific or merely fashionable. Do not call this consumer recognition.

### Campaign systems and temporary exceptions

- Campaign variation needs its own record: inherited invariants, temporary
  variables, key visual/asset family, format adaptation, rollout phases,
  localization, approval, start/end or review date, and reversion/retirement.
- A campaign should not create new “brand colours,” unapproved marks, or a new
  visual language simply because its key visual is attractive. Conversely,
  governance should not flatten every campaign into the same template.
- The identity relationship must be demonstrated across the rollout, not only
  on the hero asset. A campaign exception without an expiry or owner becomes
  permanent drift.

### Governance, versioning, and adoption

- The leaf asks governance questions but does not require one canonical asset
  registry, stable asset IDs/names, owner, approval state, version/date, change
  rationale, supersedes/deprecation relation, distribution location, and
  exception route.
- Guidelines need change control: proposed -> reviewed -> approved -> released
  -> deprecated/retired. Do not overwrite released assets without a migration
  and consumer/touchpoint risk decision.
- Design-token files can carry aliases, descriptions, types, groups, and
  deprecation metadata, but the DTCG format is an interchange contract—not a
  complete brand, product design system, or governance process (A-BV-10).
- Governance must be usable by ordinary recipients: approved files, templates,
  examples, rights status, contact/guardian, missing-data behavior, and human
  review before external release. A long PDF alone does not enforce anything.

### Accessibility

- WCAG's logotype exception must be stated accurately: text that is part of a
  logo/brand name is exempt from SC 1.4.3, but the rest of corporate identity
  and brand-guideline use is not. An interactive logo may still need an
  accessible equivalent or usable variant (A-BV-09).
- A brand palette is not “WCAG compliant” in isolation. Test specific foreground
  and background roles, states, sizes, media, and actual implementation; keep
  critical meaning available without colour alone.
- Accessible name, alternative text or decorative treatment, focus/state
  semantics, reduced motion, writing-system behavior, and physical-media needs
  route to the appropriate expert/authority. Brand cannot self-certify global
  accessibility.

### Boundary to product UI design systems

- Brand owns identity thesis, approved mark/assets, signature cues, identity
  colour/type/image/motion roles, and allowed variation across touchpoints.
- A product UI design system owns semantic tokens, component anatomy and
  states, interaction behavior, density, workflow, platform/framework rules,
  implementation, accessibility conformance, and release compatibility.
- The handoff should map brand roles to candidate semantic aliases without
  making the signature colour the default CTA, focus, success, warning, or
  error colour. UI or the incumbent framework validates those mappings.
- A DTCG token file can exchange values between tools; it does not decide the
  brand strategy or component behavior (A-BV-10).

### Asset, IP, and trademark escalation

- The leaf records legal/rights review but needs explicit prohibitions: never
  state that a proposed name/mark is clear, registrable, registered, owned, or
  licensed without current evidence from the responsible jurisdiction and
  owner.
- Clearance includes similar appearance, sound, meaning, and commercial
  impression in related goods/services; exact-match image search is not legal
  clearance. WIPO recommends broader searches and professional interpretation,
  and USPTO guidance is U.S.-specific (A-BV-11, A-BV-12).
- Font, photo, illustration, icon, template, stock, generated asset, and
  partner-mark permissions are separate from permission to learn from a style
  guide. Record each asset's source and permitted use.
- Co-branding, endorsement, trademark notices, culturally protected symbols,
  regulated marks, and contested similarities require the brand owner and
  appropriate legal/local authority.

### Causal diagnosis, repair, and proof

The current parent-cause rule needs an explicit chain:

`failure -> observed cross-touchpoint pattern -> owning invariant/variable,
asset, token, rule, approval, or distribution cause -> smallest system repair ->
affected-asset migration -> rerender/retest -> approval/evidence state`.

Without this chain, a “consistency” repair can wrongly normalize a deliberate
campaign, while a local patch can leave the broken master untouched.

## 4. Rule-quality audit

| Class | Rules that belong | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binding constraints | Preserve verified name, claims, approved assets, owners, rights, approvals, accessibility floors, incumbent systems, partner/co-brand rules, and current channel/production specifications. Never promote a proposal to approved/registered/cleared status. | Binding only from the actual brand owner, contract, asset record, applicable standard, platform, supplier, or legal authority. |
| Evidence-bounded rules | Consumer recognition and element strength require appropriate participant data; consistent use can support learned associations; major identity change can disrupt them; specific WCAG criteria apply in exact web scope. | No observed participant evidence means no recognition, recall, trust, conversion, or uniqueness claim. The cited brand-memory studies do not prove one visual style. |
| Contextual conventions | Primary/secondary marks, lockups, clear space, minimum size, positive/reversed/one-colour variants, style guides, brand books, asset libraries, parent templates, and brand guardians. | Select only conventions needed by actual touchpoints, media, organization, and governance capacity. |
| Heuristics or numeric starting points | A small set of principles, several materially different touchpoints, provisional clear space, candidate minimum sizes, and a compact colour/type system can start exploration. | Every count or number must be marked incumbent, tested, channel-specific, source-specific, or provisional. Wikimedia, Google, WCAG, printer, and app-platform values are not interchangeable. |
| Preferences | Fixed versus dynamic identity; mark-led versus type/colour/image-led recognition; rigid versus permissive governance; campaign freedom; palette size; logo visibility; construction style. | Tie preference to desired effect, production, audience, and comparison. Do not call it a defect without a violated constraint or observed failure. |
| Justified exceptions | Temporary campaign departures, co-brand lockups, small-context marks, one-colour/reversed variants, localization, platform adaptations, accessible substitutes, or emergency production substitutions. | Record purpose, inherited invariants, affected contexts, compensation, owner/approval, expiry/review, and migration/reversion. Functional, rights, and access floors survive. |

### Slogans, cargo-cult numbers, and unsupported universals to reject

- “Consistency builds trust” or “this looks recognizable” without measured
  audience evidence.
- “A good logo must work at 16 px,” “always use a construction grid,” “every
  brand needs five lockups,” or “all marks must have the same clear space.”
- “Simple logos are more memorable,” “complex logos look premium,” “natural and
  harmonious logos perform best,” or “distinctive silhouette equals
  recognition” without the exact study objective, exposure, context, and
  population.
- “Use one accent,” “maximum two typefaces,” “five to eight colours,” “an
  8-point system,” “three hierarchy levels,” or any fixed brand-manual outline.
- “The palette is WCAG compliant.” Conformance belongs to tested uses; WCAG also
  exempts some logo text while not exempting the surrounding identity.
- “Brand equals logo” and the inverse slogan “the logo does not matter.”
- “Recognition without the logo” treated as a factual result rather than a
  reviewer hypothesis or participant test.
- “Follow the guidelines exactly” when the guidelines themselves are stale,
  unapproved, inaccessible, or conflict with a higher production/legal owner.
- “Trademark search passed” based on exact visual search, model memory, or a
  single registry.
- “Brand tokens are the product design system.” Tokens can exchange values;
  they do not own components, states, interaction, or strategy.

### Contradiction and gap resolution

- Public Skills repeatedly prescribe 16 px, 48 px, fixed clear-space formulas,
  one accent, an 8-point grid, or fixed variant counts. A-BV-07 and A-BV-08
  demonstrate that numeric requirements belong to a specific mark and channel.
  Result: require tests and authority, not universal values.
- A-BV-04 shows that professional intuition often misjudges consumer brand
  memory; A-BV-05 and A-BV-06 show that design effects depend on objective,
  exposure, population, and variation. Result: reviewer judgment can diagnose a
  system but cannot certify recognition.
- WCAG exempts logotype text from one contrast criterion while public Skills
  often demand that every logo pass ordinary text contrast. Result: preserve
  the exact exception but require usable variants/equivalents and test all
  non-logo brand communication in applicable scope.
- DTCG supplies a stable token exchange format but explicitly does not supply
  identity semantics or organizational governance. Result: token validation is
  one technical proof, not a brand-system result.
- The current module excludes trademark clearance; A-BV-11 and A-BV-12 show why
  exact-match visual search is insufficient. Result: record status and escalate
  rather than implying clearance.

## 8. Architecture recommendation

### Decision: retain one leaf and make coverage complete before compression

The Brand module should remain a `focus` leaf and retain maturity status `stub`
until open Terra generation, critique, repair, production, and owner/audience
evidence exists. The candidate itself should nevertheless cover every
inseparable professional floor identified here: identity frame, evidence state,
authority, mark/application stress, campaign lifecycle, governance, causal
repair, and external verification. Maturity labels limit claims; they must not
force an incomplete instruction set.

The 1,800-token figure is a cost target, not an admission or correctness gate.
Draft the smallest leaf that preserves the following complete structure, record
its actual token and read-graph cost, test it, and compress only where removal
does not reduce discrimination, repair, ownership, or proof. Exceed 1,800 when
the tested professional floor cannot be expressed safely within it:

1. **Authority and status:** verified strategy/name/claims; canonical assets and
   owners; proposed/approved/released/deprecated/cleared/registered states;
   missing decisions and approvers.
2. **Identity grammar:** thesis; primary identifiers; signature/supporting
   cues; invariant versus variable elements; allowed transformations and
   fallbacks; no recognition claim without participants.
3. **Mark/application matrix:** only required variants; actual minimum-size,
   one-colour, reversed, favicon/platform, co-brand, localization, and
   production contexts; route mark redesign and current technical specs.
4. **Touchpoint and campaign stress:** materially different real-content
   extremes; campaign inheritance, temporary variables, expiry, and retirement.
5. **Governance:** canonical registry/source of truth, version/status,
   approval, rights, deprecation/migration, distribution, exception path, and
   Brand-to-UI/production handoff.
6. **Critique/repair/proof:** recognition hypothesis, specificity, drift versus
   dead uniformity, accessibility, asset sprawl, unsupported status; follow the
   failure-to-parent-cause chain; rerender and report exact evidence.
7. **Ceiling:** no strategy, naming, architecture, consumer-recognition,
   trademark, stakeholder, mark-specialist, product-UI, or production-ready
   claim without the relevant owner/evidence.

Source-map changes, if later authorized, should retain L-18, L-19, L-21, and
L-22; add L-14/L-17 for scoped accessibility, a current verified mark/platform
case only as an example, A-BV-04 for recognition evidence limits, and WIPO or
jurisdiction-specific current sources for legal escalation. DTCG should support
technical token interchange only, not define brand ownership.

The load-bearing assumption challenged here is that expert visual judgment can
identify a brand's strongest recognizable assets. A-BV-04 shows that
professionals' intuition often diverges from consumer memory. The architecture
therefore keeps recognition research outside the stub and turns “recognition
without the logo” into a bounded diagnostic hypothesis.

## 9. Tests and claim ceiling

### Smallest open Terra High falsification set

Use paired current-stub versus candidate-stub arms with the same Terra High
model, reasoning, public fixtures, co-loaded experts, renderers, and
deterministic checks. Blind human reviewers to the arm. Keep these cases open;
do not touch sealed holdout material.

| Case | Minimal public task | What would falsify the proposed change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generation: approved identity system rollout | Given an approved fictional strategy/name, canonical primary mark SVG, licensed type/assets, exact claims, owner matrix, and target specs, define the identity grammar and create/render a small-context favicon/header, one-colour stamp, bilingual campaign/social card, partner co-brand lockup, and dense document cover. Include only needed variants, a canonical asset/status registry, and Brand-to-UI/production handoff. | Candidate invents strategy/approvals/clearance; produces generic uniform templates; imports fixed logo rules; loses mark/content; fails one-colour/small/co-brand/localized contexts; conflates brand and UI semantic tokens; or adds no useful result over current. |
| Critique: recognition, accessibility, and governance | Audit a supplied identity pack with one strong non-logo cue, one merely fashionable cue, two conflicting logo masters, a stale campaign variant with no expiry, an inaccessible non-logo text pairing, a false “trademark cleared” note, and a UI status colour mapped directly to the brand accent. No edit. | Candidate claims consumer recognition, misses status/rights/access/ownership failures, scores preference as fact, recommends replacing the strong learned cue without evidence, or cannot identify the parent governance causes. |
| Repair: canonical parent cause | Repair a six-touchpoint family where a campaign graphic and obsolete mark became accidental invariants because a deprecated manifest entry remains canonical. Preserve the approved primary mark, name, claims, strong signature cue, and documented campaign variation; update the registry/migration and rerender affected contexts only. | Candidate patches every artifact, redesigns the mark, erases controlled variation, changes strategy, leaves stale references, makes unsupported legal claims, or reports completion without before/after family and version evidence. |

### Deterministic checks

- Exact preservation of supplied name, claims, required text, owners, approved
  asset hashes/IDs, rights statements, and target dimensions.
- No new `approved`, `released`, `cleared`, `registered`, `owned`, or
  `production-ready` status without a supplied authority receipt.
- Every asset registry entry has stable ID/path, owner/source, version/date,
  state, scope, approval/evidence, rights status, and supersedes/deprecation
  relation where applicable; no released consumer references a retired asset.
- Mark/source validity: parseable self-contained SVG or declared format,
  expected view box/artboard/transparency, no missing resource, and exact
  approved colour/background variant.
- Render the fixture's supplied small, one-colour, reversed, large/distance,
  responsive/localized, content-dense, and co-brand contexts; test clipping,
  lost required details, clear-space/placement values from the fixture, and
  partner-emphasis rules.
- Test actual non-logo text/background and UI role pairs against the applicable
  WCAG fixture; verify colour is not the only critical distinction. Do not fail
  exempt logotype text under SC 1.4.3 automatically.
- If design tokens are requested, validate the exact declared format/version,
  references, types, aliases, and deprecation metadata; do not infer brand or UI
  semantics from token groups.
- Campaign variables include owner, scope, inherited anchors, start/end or
  review, and retirement/reversion; expired variants are not presented as
  canonical.
- Repair diff changes the canonical manifest/rule/alias and only the affected
  consumers; unrelated artifacts and deliberate variants remain byte- or
  render-equivalent where the format permits.
- Candidate retains the module ID/signal/owners and does not create
  dependencies or flat-leaf routing not justified by the test. Record token and
  read-graph cost, but do not fail an otherwise coverage-correct leaf solely for
  exceeding 1,800 tokens; compression requires a no-regression ablation.

Deterministic checks can prove content, file, status, registry, geometry,
contrast-scope, and migration facts. They cannot prove that an identity is
distinctive, recognizable, appropriate, trusted, preferred, legally available,
or strategically correct.

### Required render or production evidence

- Generation: one same-scale overview of the family plus exact-size renders of
  every target context; actual one-colour/reversed output; favicon/platform
  preview only under the supplied current contract; localization and co-brand
  comparison; source/registry validation receipt.
- Critique: supplied artifacts, source masters, manifests/guidelines, and
  rendered contexts inspected; findings point to evidence locations and keep
  proposals separate from approvals.
- Repair: before/after family overview and same-scale affected touchpoints;
  unchanged-reference receipt for preserved assets; new manifest/deprecation
  and consumer-reference checks.
- Physical print, embroidery, signage, colour, app-store, partner, or platform
  readiness requires the actual supplier/platform proof or acceptance. A
  generic render does not production-qualify a variant.

### Human and domain authority

- Brand owner/guardian approves identity thesis, invariants, variants,
  campaign exception, migration, and release.
- Target category participants or customers are required for recognition,
  attribution, fame/uniqueness, preference, or comprehension claims. One
  designer review is not a proxy.
- At least one qualified design reviewer can judge family cohesion,
  specificity, controlled variation, and visible reproduction defects; a
  second reviewer/disagreement record is required before claiming consensus.
- Accessibility specialist and native reader are required when consequential
  media, scripts, localization, or equivalents exceed the inspected evidence.
- Printer/fabricator/platform/venue/partner owns production, safe area,
  co-brand, colour, and acceptance requirements.
- Trademark counsel/agent or responsible jurisdictional authority owns
  clearance, registrability, registration, notices, classes, territories, and
  conflict interpretation.

### Admission and claim ceiling

Do not promote the module from `stub` because one case passes. Admit only the
smallest rule cluster that improves repeated or cross-case behavior without
regressing model strengths, routing, creative variation, or ownership. Preserve
original receipts when harness defects are corrected.

Even passing all three cases would support only a source-bound identity-system
process and the exact verified artifacts. It would not support:

- brand strategy, naming, positioning, architecture, messaging, or business
  outcome expertise;
- specialist logo/mark design or construction mastery;
- consumer recognition, recall, attribution, trust, preference, conversion,
  or brand equity without participant evidence;
- trademark availability, registrability, ownership, non-infringement, or
  clearance in any jurisdiction;
- stakeholder alignment or organizational adoption beyond recorded approvals;
- global accessibility, multilingual, cultural, production, or platform
  readiness;
- universal minimum sizes, clear space, variant counts, palette/type limits,
  visual rules, or governance cadence;
- a product UI design system or implemented components from brand tokens;
- independent or original Brainstorm generation.
