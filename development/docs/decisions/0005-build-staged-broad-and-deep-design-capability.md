# Build a staged broad-and-deep design capability

## Decision

Build Scoville Design as one broad, standalone design authority with a deeply
qualified shared core and task-routed specialist knowledge. Cover the complete
professional competency map, but claim a specialist domain only after its
source base, production constraints, behavior, and visual outcomes have been
qualified. Compare the Skill against the strongest relevant public Skills on
frozen cases. Treat "best on the market" as the development objective, not as
a public superlative unless controlled evidence supports that exact claim.

The staged architecture combines three layers: a thin artifact contract and
multi-route router; a generative design-and-judgment cycle from brief through
rendered revision; and cross-media stress tests for content, context,
localization, accessibility, sequence, production, and provenance.

## Problem

A narrow Skill cannot serve as an allround graphic designer. A broad but
shallow checklist only appears comprehensive and can produce unsafe specialist
advice. A monolithic prompt would waste context and reduce routing precision.
The Skill needs breadth, real generative ability, critical judgment, and honest
qualification without pretending that every production domain is equally
mature on day one.

## Drivers

- The explicit objective is the strongest design Skill available, not a minor
  extension of the existing UI Skill.
- Designing and critiquing require a complete creative cycle, not only defect
  detection.
- Professional competence includes semiotics, information design, motion and
  sequence, cultural and ethical judgment, production, collaboration,
  localization, accessibility, and asset provenance in addition to visual
  craft.
- Progressive disclosure must keep broad knowledge affordable in context.
- Market-leading claims require comparative and visual evidence.

## Considered alternatives

- Build a broad shallow encyclopedia. This maximizes labels but not usable
  judgment or production safety.
- Build only a deep typography and composition specialist. This is easier to
  qualify but does not meet the allround-designer objective.
- Put every competency in one Core prompt. This weakens discovery, focus, and
  token discipline.
- Split each design field into a required sibling Skill. This violates the
  standalone requirement and creates routing and installation dependencies.

## Consequences

- The audit and evaluation matrix must expose unqualified domains rather than
  hiding them behind a general claim.
- Generation, critique, and revision receive separate cases and evidence.
- Specialist references remain task-routed and can mature independently.
- Qualification includes no-Skill and relevant public-Skill comparators, with
  benchmark defects adjudicated separately from Skill defects.
- Human visual judgment at the intended size and context remains necessary;
  automated or model-based aesthetic scores are supporting evidence only.

## Confirmation

The repository contains a complete competency map, qualified-domain ledger,
frozen comparator matrix, and progressive-disclosure traces. Representative
tasks show the Skill generating, explaining, critiquing, and revising original
designs rather than merely reciting rules. Each claimed domain has source,
behavioral, rendered, and human-review evidence, and public claims are no
broader than those results.

## Revisit when

The broad router causes material context or ownership failures, specialist
domains cannot be qualified without dedicated Skills, or controlled evidence
shows that a different architecture produces stronger outcomes.
